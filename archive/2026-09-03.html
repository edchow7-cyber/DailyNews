U.N. probe says foreign support is fueling Sudan war crimes

A U.N. fact-finding mission says foreign military support, including drones, weapons and personnel, is helping sustain Sudan’s war and may be contributing to war crimes by both major factions. More than 14 million people have been displaced since fighting began in 2023, and the investigators say drone attacks killed more than 1,000 people in the first half of this year. The report adds pressure for broader enforcement of the arms embargo as outside support makes an already catastrophic conflict harder to contain.

Nvidia buys Hugging Face for nearly $13 billion

Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for about $12.93 billion, giving the chipmaker control of one of the most important platforms for distributing and collaborating on open AI models. Strategically, the deal helps Nvidia hedge against large customers developing their own chips while tying the company more closely to the open-model ecosystem used by enterprises and researchers.

Thomson Reuters discloses breach of court-record platform

Thomson Reuters says an unauthorized party accessed files connected to its C-Track court-management platform, which is used by courts in 11 U.S. states, the U.S. Virgin Islands and Ontario. The company detected the incident in June and says the unauthorized access occurred earlier in the year; affected material included court records containing names and other personal information. Story 1 of 3

California moves to restrict lawyers’ use of confidential data in AI

California lawmakers have passed SB 574, which would require lawyers to verify AI-generated work, disclose certain AI use in court filings and avoid entering confidential or nonpublic information into generative-AI systems. The measure now awaits the governor’s signature and effectively turns existing professional duties into explicit operational requirements for AI workflows. It is another example of regulated industries moving beyond broad AI principles toward concrete rules governing what data may be exposed to a model.

Why it matters to Nera: A rule that says “do not put confidential data into the model” creates a direct barrier to useful AI adoption in high-value workflows. Privacy-preserving computation can change that equation by allowing sensitive information to remain protected while still supporting analysis.

→ Reuters — California lawmakers pass bill governing lawyers’ use of AI

Story 2 of 3

Uber drivers challenge algorithmic pay under European privacy law

Drivers in several European countries have launched a class action challenging Uber’s use of algorithms to set pay and assign jobs. The case argues that the platform’s “black box” decision-making uses personal data in ways that violate GDPR protections and can result in different drivers receiving different offers for comparable work. Whether or not the plaintiffs ultimately prevail, the dispute shows algorithmic decision systems becoming a data-governance issue as much as a labor or fairness issue.

Why it matters to Nera: As regulators scrutinize how data feeds automated decisions, enterprises will need stronger separation between sensitive inputs, model execution and auditable outputs. That expands the design space for PETs beyond simple data storage or transfer.

→ The Guardian — Uber drivers challenge algorithmic pay under GDPR

Story 3 of 3

Court-data breach underscores the value of reducing plaintext exposure

The Thomson Reuters C-Track incident exposed files from a platform used across multiple court systems, showing the blast radius created when sensitive data is concentrated behind shared infrastructure. Traditional cybersecurity controls remain essential, but they do not eliminate the consequences once an attacker reaches authorized or decrypted data. Architectures that minimize where plaintext exists can reduce what a successful intrusion is able to reveal.

Why it matters to Nera: FHE does not replace identity, endpoint or network security, but it can reduce the amount of sensitive data that must ever be exposed during computation. That “assume breach, minimize plaintext” framing is increasingly understandable to enterprise buyers after incidents like this.

→ Reuters — Thomson Reuters C-Track breach

Story 3 of 3

Gray wolf is confirmed just north of the Bay Area

California wildlife officials have confirmed a gray wolf in rural Lake County near Mendocino National Forest, one of the rare modern sightings west of Interstate 5. Officials estimate the black wolf is three or four years old and are setting up cameras to determine whether it is simply passing through or could help establish a new pack in the Coast Range. California now has 11 known packs and an estimated 50 to 75 wolves, making the Lake County sighting another sign of the species’ continuing return after nearly a century of absence.
