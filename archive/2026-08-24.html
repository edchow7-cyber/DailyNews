New U.S. sanctions on Iran put Hormuz back at center stage

Global markets opened Monday bracing for another major U.S. sanctions package aimed at Iran, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expected to demand that Tehran give up control of the Strait of Hormuz. Oil prices fell about 2% ahead of the announcement, but the core risk is escalation: any Iranian response that further restricts shipping could quickly reverse that move and reintroduce an energy shock. The sanctions campaign is becoming the primary U.S. lever as military options appear less decisive, making the next phase a test of whether economic pressure produces negotiations or retaliation.

U.S.–Canada trade dispute escalates after talks collapse

Canada is preparing dollar-for-dollar retaliation after trade negotiations with Washington failed and 50% U.S. tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian imports remained in place. The dispute matters because the two economies share deeply integrated supply chains, so tariffs can rapidly spill into manufacturing costs, consumer prices and investment decisions on both sides of the border. With U.S. midterm elections approaching, the risk is that the confrontation becomes politically harder to unwind even if businesses on both sides push for a compromise.

Inherent says a smaller agent beat frontier models at research replication

London startup Inherent says its Faraday agent outperformed much larger frontier systems from Anthropic and OpenAI on a task that required independently reproducing published scientific results. The notable part is efficiency: Faraday runs on a 27-billion-parameter Qwen model and relies heavily on reinforcement learning and an agentic workflow rather than simply scaling the base model. If the result generalizes, it supports a growing view that the agent harness, training method and task design can matter as much as raw model size for specialized work.

Story 2 of 3

OpenAI now wants California to strengthen its AI safety law

OpenAI is urging California to add stronger safeguards to SB 53, including monitoring frontier models during training and evaluation and tighter cybersecurity requirements throughout the model-development lifecycle. That is a notable reversal from the company’s earlier opposition to the law, and it comes after recent incidents raised questions about whether frontier labs can reliably contain dangerous behavior. OpenAI is also explicitly arguing that state-level rules may become the foundation for a future national framework if federal legislation remains stalled.

Story 3 of 3

Flock backlash turns surveillance governance into a product problem

Flock Safety is trying to address growing public and government backlash over its license-plate surveillance network by tightening retention recommendations and adding controls around how police customers preserve and share data. The company now recommends seven-day default retention and has introduced an “Evidence Mode” that requires a specific case number for longer storage. The broader lesson is that privacy concerns are increasingly shaping product architecture itself, not just legal disclaimers — especially for systems that centralize sensitive behavioral data.

Story 1 of 3

California’s DROP system makes privacy compliance operational

California data brokers are now required to retrieve and process centralized deletion requests through the state’s Delete Request and Opt-out Platform at least every 45 days. CalPrivacy has paired the new workflow with fresh enforcement actions, making clear that these are recurring data-processing obligations rather than policy statements. The direction of travel is important: privacy regulation is moving deeper into the mechanics of how companies store, share, transform and delete data.

Why it matters to Nera: This strengthens the case for privacy infrastructure that can minimize exposure and make controls technically enforceable and auditable, rather than relying on downstream policy compliance alone.

Duality packages PETs with sovereign enterprise infrastructure

Duality is positioning privacy-preserving collaboration as part of sovereign enterprise AI architecture rather than a standalone cryptography layer. Its work with IBM Sovereign Core is designed to keep identity, keys, logs and operational control inside a customer-controlled sovereign boundary while still allowing approved analytics and AI workloads to operate across sensitive distributed data. That framing brings PETs closer to established enterprise buying categories such as data sovereignty, regulated AI and secure infrastructure.

Why it matters to Nera: Duality is a useful competitive benchmark for how PET vendors can attach themselves to large infrastructure budgets. Nera’s positioning should keep emphasizing where confidential computation produces a simpler or more economically compelling workflow for finance and analytics teams.

Zama keeps pushing FHE into deployable financial products

Zama continues to commercialize fully homomorphic encryption through application-layer financial products rather than presenting FHE only as infrastructure. Its confidential USDC vault with Morpho and Steakhouse Financial encrypts individual positions while keeping the underlying strategy auditable, and its partnership with Elliptic adds compliance screening to confidential finance. That combination of privacy plus familiar financial and compliance primitives is designed to make FHE easier for institutions to adopt.

Why it matters to Nera: Zama is demonstrating an application-led route to market: make confidentiality invisible inside a concrete business workflow and pair it with compliance tooling buyers already understand.

Bay Area stays relatively mild while Southern California bakes

Southern California is entering a potentially record-breaking heat wave, with some inland locations forecast to approach or exceed 110°F. The Bay Area is largely escaping the worst of it, with coastal areas staying in the 60s and 70s and inland communities seeing only a brief midweek rise into the 90s. Haze and wildfire smoke may still affect visibility and air quality at times, but the region’s marine influence is keeping the overall pattern much more moderate.

Oakland port tour becomes an unlikely summer hit

A free 90-minute narrated ferry tour of the Port of Oakland has become one of the Bay Area’s hardest summer tickets to get, with reservations disappearing within minutes. The tour gives visitors an unusually close look at container operations, cranes, ship logistics and the broader economic role of the port. It is a lighter local story, but a useful reminder that one of the region’s most consequential pieces of infrastructure is usually almost invisible to people who live around it.
