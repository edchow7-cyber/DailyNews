Investigators examine icing reports after fatal Florida Learjet crash

A Learjet 60 carrying nine people crashed near a busy Palm Beach County road Sunday, killing everyone aboard but apparently missing nearby homes and motorists. Before the crash, the crew reported icing, turbulence, and precipitation, then declared an emergency as the aircraft entered an extreme dive. About 600 residents were evacuated while federal investigators secured the scene. The NTSB will examine weather, aircraft performance, and crew response before determining a cause.

Tech

Ivanti patches two more zero-days used in a three-flaw attack chain

Ivanti disclosed and patched two Connect Secure vulnerabilities that attackers chained with an authentication-bypass flaw disclosed in October. The combined chain allowed unauthenticated root-level code execution even against a fully patched appliance at the time of exploitation. CISA added the newly disclosed flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and set remediation deadlines for federal agencies. The incident is another warning that perimeter devices require rapid patching, credential rotation, log review, and sometimes full rebuilds after compromise.

Source: SecurityWeek, November 2

AI coding agents need their own identity, narrow credentials, and enforced boundaries

A new security interview argues that agents should never simply borrow a human user's login. Business agents should run under scoped identities, while human-directed agents should carry both the agent identity and the requester's identity for authorization and audit. The recommended baseline also separates proposing a change from applying it, brokers short-lived credentials, denies broad network access, and blocks protected-branch or infrastructure mutations without review. The larger lesson is that policy must be enforced by the surrounding system rather than entrusted to model instructions alone.

Source: Help Net Security, November 2

Nera Watch

New York bans manipulative AI-companion behavior toward minors

New York's new law bars youth-facing chatbots from presenting themselves as real people with emotions or relationships, encouraging secrecy, or sending sexually explicit or manipulative messages. The attorney general can pursue damages, shutdowns, and data destruction, and the law takes effect January 1. The measure moves beyond disclosure requirements toward regulating product behavior and data practices directly. It also signals that state-by-state AI rules will continue to fragment while federal policy remains unsettled.

Why this is important to Nera: Customers using confidential AI will still need enforceable product controls, audit trails, and proof that sensitive youth data is not repurposed. Nera can differentiate by pairing cryptographic privacy with policy enforcement and verifiable data-use boundaries, while treating regulated chatbot vendors as both prospects and high-risk deployment partners.

Source: Associated Press, October 29

Microsoft makes school AI privacy protections contractually available nationwide

Beginning November 1, U.S. school districts can ask Microsoft to add a national AI safety and privacy standard to new or existing agreements. The provisions restrict use of student and educator data for model training or advertising, require human oversight, and promise clearer transparency for families. Contractual terms give districts a stronger enforcement mechanism than voluntary principles alone. Adoption will test whether vendors can translate broad privacy promises into operational controls, retention rules, and auditable workflows.

Why this is important to Nera: Education is a strong confidential-AI use case because schools need analytics and assistance without exposing student records. Nera should map its FHE-based processing and data-minimization story directly to these contract clauses, giving districts and technology partners evidence that protected data can remain encrypted during computation.

Source: Microsoft, effective November 1

California privacy regulators prepare November 5–6 rulemaking discussions

The California Privacy Protection Agency has posted materials for this week's board meeting, including regulation updates, proposed rule text, and recommendations on reducing friction and improving notices and disclosures. Existing rules already cover cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and consumer rights involving automated decision-making technology, with phased compliance deadlines beginning in 2027. The meeting provides an early signal about how the agency will refine implementation and enforcement. California's approach often shapes national product requirements because companies prefer one high-standard operating model.

Why this is important to Nera: Risk assessments and meaningful explanations create a sales opening for privacy-preserving analytics that can demonstrate minimized access to raw data. Nera should track the meeting for language affecting automated decisions, audit evidence, deletion, and disclosures, then translate it into pilot controls and compliance-ready customer documentation.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency

Retrial begins in the 2012 Sierra LaMar disappearance

Opening statements are scheduled Monday in the retrial of Antolin Garcia-Torres, accused of killing 15-year-old Sierra LaMar after she disappeared on her way to school in Morgan Hill. His 2017 conviction was overturned this year after an appellate court found that joining the murder case with three earlier attempted-kidnapping charges caused improper cross-admission of evidence. Prosecutors again face a no-body murder case, but some prior theories of premeditation are now barred. The retrial revisits one of Santa Clara County's most enduring and painful missing-person cases.

Source: CBS Bay Area, November 1
