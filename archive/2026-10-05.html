Supreme Court opens a term spanning climate suits, immigration, guns and voting

The Supreme Court begins its new term Monday with disputes that could materially reshape climate accountability, immigration detention, assault-weapon restrictions and election rules. The opening climate case asks whether ExxonMobil and Suncor can block Boulder’s state-law claims over alleged deception about fossil-fuel harms. Later cases test third-country deportations, proof-of-citizenship rules and government treatment of a religious preschool with discriminatory admissions policies. The breadth of the docket means the court’s conservative majority will again set national policy far beyond the facts of any one lawsuit.

Norway plans a temporary ban on AI glasses in selected public places

Norway’s government says it will propose temporarily barring AI-enabled smart glasses in some public spaces because bystanders may be photographed, recorded or transcribed without meaningful notice. The move targets the ambient-capture capability rather than banning wearable computing outright. It places consent, visible signaling and purpose limitation ahead of a complete long-term regulatory framework. The proposal is an early test of whether governments will regulate AI wearables as cameras, biometric systems, assistants or all three at once.
