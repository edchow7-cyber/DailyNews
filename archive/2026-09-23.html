Hurricane Polo’s rainbands reach Mexico with a major flood threat

Outer rainbands from Hurricane Polo reached Mexico’s Pacific coast Wednesday after the storm briefly intensified to Category 5 and then weakened while remaining a major hurricane. The core was forecast to stay offshore, but Guerrero and Michoacán could receive 3 to 8 inches of rain. The National Hurricane Center warned that the prolonged downpour could trigger life-threatening flash floods and mudslides. Mexican authorities opened shelters and urged residents in flood-prone areas to identify routes to higher ground.

Super El Niño could cause up to 451,000 excess heat deaths

A Climate Impact Lab analysis estimates that an exceptionally strong El Niño could contribute to as many as 451,000 excess heat deaths worldwide over the next six months. Researchers project that the number of extremely hot days could rise by roughly 44%, with lower-income countries facing the greatest health burden. Nigeria, Indonesia and Sudan are among the countries projected to experience especially high mortality. The findings strengthen the case for heat alerts, cooling access and emergency planning before peak temperatures arrive.

EU renews Russia sanctions while removing two prominent billionaires

The European Union renewed sanctions on roughly 3,000 Russian people and entities for three years, replacing the previous six-month renewal cycle. Ukraine condemned the removals, while Latvia said it may impose national restrictions of its own. The agreement keeps the wider asset-freeze and travel-ban regime in force while exposing continued divisions inside the EU over enforcement.

Jet-powered Russian drones strain Ukraine’s air defenses

Russia’s newer jet-powered attack drones are flying faster and higher than propeller-driven Shaheds, making them harder for Ukraine’s mobile teams to intercept. Ukrainian officials told Reuters the interception rate for the jet variants is about 60%, compared with roughly 90% for older models. The drones are increasingly targeting ports, railways and steel plants as Kyiv faces a large defense-funding gap. Ukraine is racing to scale interceptor drones and other lower-cost countermeasures before the tactic expands further.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington and Beijing agreed to extend their trade truce through January 10 as President Donald Trump welcomed Chinese President Xi Jinping for a three-day visit. Saudi Arabia restarts a pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz

Saudi Arabia resumed low-rate operations on its East-West oil pipeline after drone attacks shut it and halted loadings at Yanbu on September 13, sources told Reuters. The route can carry up to 4 million barrels per day—about 4% of global supply—from eastern fields to the Red Sea, bypassing the disrupted Strait of Hormuz. Operators are working toward full capacity while tankers reposition for renewed exports and ship-to-ship transfers. Brent crude fell more than $2 as the restart eased immediate supply concerns.

→ Reuters — Saudi Arabia restarts East-West oil pipeline

Gulf energy shock could drive a 70% jump in global biofuel output

A Chatham House and Forest Stewardship Council study projects global biofuel production could rise nearly 70% from 2025 levels by 2030 as governments respond to energy-security concerns and higher crude prices. The report warns that rapid expansion of first-generation fuels made from food crops could intensify food insecurity and land and water pressure. It estimates additional demand could require as much as 36 million hectares of land. Waste-based fuels offer a lower-impact path, but the study says policy safeguards and certification systems are not keeping pace.

South Korea laid out a plan to cut the Middle East share of its crude-oil imports to 50% by 2035, Reuters reported Wednesday. The target is a long-term industrial-policy response to the supply disruptions and price volatility exposed this year.

EU proposes energy and water disclosure labels for data centers

The European Commission proposed rules requiring large data centers to disclose standardized measures of energy and water efficiency. Facilities above 500 kilowatts would also report how their water use relates to local stress and whether they can reuse waste heat or support the surrounding power grid. The proposal does not cap consumption, but the label could become a foundation for mandatory minimum standards. Europe expects data-center capacity to more than double by 2030 as it expands AI infrastructure.

Banks warn AI shopping agents could amplify fraud and privacy risks

A coalition including Bank of America, Capital One, NatWest, ING and Commonwealth Bank of Australia warned that AI shopping agents are advancing faster than consumer-protection standards. For privacy-enhancing technology vendors, the report points directly to demand for controlled data sharing, auditable agent actions and minimized payment-data exposure.

Failed transit measures could more than double peak Bay Bridge delays

A SPUR-commissioned analysis says deep BART, Muni and Caltrain service cuts could push enough commuters into cars to more than double peak Bay Bridge travel times.
