Russia plans a 27% increase in 2027 military spending

Russian budget documents show planned 2027 defense spending of 17.1 trillion roubles, about $203 billion and 27% above the amount originally budgeted. The figure would be the highest since the war in Ukraine began, with roughly 50 trillion roubles projected across three years. Financing the increase is pushing Moscow toward higher borrowing, tax increases and greater use of its sovereign wealth fund. The plan signals that the Kremlin is preparing for a prolonged, resource-intensive conflict rather than a near-term wind-down.

Russian jet-drone strikes kill 10 across Ukraine

Russian jet-powered drone attacks killed 10 people across Ukraine and injured more than 80, according to Ukrainian officials. Two people died when Kyiv’s National Academy of Sciences was struck, while attacks also hit a Dnipro business center and civilian infrastructure elsewhere. Ukraine said 90 of the 120 drones launched were faster jet-propelled models and that only 57% were intercepted. The changing weapon mix is raising urgent pressure for new interceptors and more adaptable air defenses.

FTC chair says AI developers should answer for agent conduct

FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson rejected the idea that AI agents should be treated as independent actors with wills of their own. He said existing law can attach responsibility to the people and companies that instruct systems, particularly when agents cause harm or gain unauthorized access to corporate or government data. The agency is also preparing a study of personalized pricing while continuing probes into ad fraud and algorithmic practices. For Nera, the signal is direct: agent deployments need attributable instructions, bounded authority and audit trails that make responsibility legible to regulators.

San Francisco reports a record-low 136 new HIV infections

San Francisco recorded 136 new HIV infections in 2025, twelve fewer than the prior year and a 75% decline over two decades. Prevention medication, rapid connection to treatment and viral suppression have driven the long decline, but city officials say progress remains uneven. Black and Latino residents and transgender women remain disproportionately represented among new cases, while housing and health-care access continue to shape risk. The city’s next challenge is extending prevention and treatment to the communities still least able to reach them.

Rare September coastal flooding could reach 1.2 feet

The Bay Area’s second-ever September coastal flood advisory is in effect through Thursday, with up to 1.2 feet of seawater inundation possible along low-lying shorelines and tidal waterways. Forecasters link the elevated water to El Niño-driven coastally trapped waves rather than a local storm. The advisory is an early warning for winter, when king tides can combine with rain runoff and wind-driven surge. Waterfront roads, trails and parking areas are the most immediate local exposure.
