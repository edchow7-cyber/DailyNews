Russian strikes kill at least 32 across Ukraine

A large Russian missile-and-drone attack killed at least 32 civilians and injured more than 100 across Ukraine on October 7. Pryluky suffered the heaviest reported toll after a residential building was hit, while Kyiv and several other cities also reported deaths and damage. Ukraine said it intercepted most of the drones but still lacks enough high-end defenses for ballistic threats. The scale of the attack puts renewed pressure on European and U.S. decisions about air-defense support.

IMF chief warns that energy, debt and AI risks are converging

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva says high energy prices, public debt and rapid AI investment are creating a difficult mix for the global economy. Governments have less fiscal room to absorb shocks while oil and possible gas disruptions continue to feed inflation. She still expects AI to add meaningfully to growth, but warned about market concentration, labor displacement and cyber risk. The message ahead of IMF and World Bank meetings is resilience, not a clean return to low-inflation expansion.

Big Tech increasingly turns to capital markets to fund the AI buildout

Major technology companies are increasingly using debt and equity offerings to finance enormous AI and cloud-infrastructure programs. Investors are now underwriting not just chips and data centers, but the assumption that future AI revenue will cover higher financing costs. Story 3 of 4

AI is changing tasks faster than it is eliminating whole jobs

ADP chief economist Nela Richardson argues that AI’s near-term labor effect is primarily task transformation rather than wholesale job elimination. Younger workers appear to be feeling the disruption more sharply, while experienced employees are proving more resilient than many forecasts assumed. That pattern suggests domain knowledge and judgment can become more valuable when routine production is automated. Employers still face a training problem: productivity gains will be uneven if entry-level workers lose the tasks through which expertise was traditionally built.

California’s CIPA overhaul may end thousands of web-tracking suits

A California law removes the private right of action from one CIPA provision that plaintiffs had used in web-tracking cases. The change applies retroactively for two years and could eliminate thousands of lawsuits and demand letters. Consumer lawyers warn that private enforcement will weaken, while businesses say the earlier wave often targeted ordinary analytics without proof of harm. Companies should not confuse lower litigation risk with permission to overcollect; CCPA duties and other surveillance claims remain.
