Power and cooling become the next AI infrastructure winners

The trillion-dollar data-center buildout is creating a second tier of beneficiaries beyond Nvidia: companies supplying electrical equipment, cooling systems, transformers and power-management technology. Data centers increasingly face physical bottlenecks in electricity delivery and heat removal, making these components just as important to deployment schedules as GPUs themselves. The investment story is therefore shifting from “who makes the chips?” toward “who can actually power and cool all of this compute?”

Story 1 of 3

CalPrivacy extends its data-broker enforcement blitz

California’s privacy regulator fined Virginia-based SalesIntel Research $36,400 for failing to register on time as a data broker and ordered changes to its practices. SalesIntel sells access to more than 200 million professional contacts and markets tools that can de-anonymize website visitors; the order also requires it to use California’s DROP system and process future deletion requests. The action extends a recent run of enforcement cases and shows California turning privacy rights into recurring operational obligations rather than one-time disclosures.

Why it matters to Nera: Enforcement is increasingly about what organizations actually do with sensitive data, not simply what their privacy policy says. That strengthens the commercial case for technical controls that reduce exposure and make data-handling guarantees provable.

Story 2 of 3

Frontier-model safety is becoming an enterprise control problem

OpenAI’s decision to apply stronger controls to Astra illustrates a broader shift: the more capable AI agents become, the more enterprises will need policy enforcement around what models can access and execute. Cybersecurity capability is particularly sensitive because the same agent can help defenders find weaknesses or help attackers exploit them. This increases demand for architectures that limit data exposure and constrain execution without eliminating the value of powerful models.

Why it matters to Nera: Confidential computation can become part of a layered control model for advanced agents, especially where sensitive financial data must be analyzed without giving either the model provider or adjacent systems plaintext access.

Story 3 of 3

Duality and IBM push sovereign AI into protected-data collaboration

IBM Sovereign Core and Duality are combining customer-operated infrastructure with privacy-enhancing technologies so organizations can analyze distributed sensitive data without exposing the raw information. The positioning goes beyond keeping data in a sovereign cloud: it emphasizes collaboration across organizations while preserving local control over identity, keys, logs and data access. That is a stronger competitive formulation of the private-AI market because it addresses the cases where the useful data cannot simply be centralized.

Why it matters to Nera: Duality is framing PETs as the missing layer between sovereign infrastructure and cross-party analytics. Nera’s finance use cases should emphasize where FHE enables exactly that kind of collaboration with fewer trust assumptions.

Story 1 of 3

New model raises Bay Area’s 30-year major-quake odds

New U.S. Geological Survey calculations put the probability of a magnitude-6.7-or-larger Bay Area earthquake over the next 30 years at 74.1%, up from 72% in the previous estimate. The Hayward–Rodgers Creek system remains the region’s most likely major source at about 34.3%, while the San Andreas risk also increased. The change is not a prediction of an imminent quake, but it is a meaningful reminder that household preparedness, retrofit work and insurance decisions remain rational rather than theoretical concerns.
