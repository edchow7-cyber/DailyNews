Top Headlines

Democrats promise to rein in Trump after their sweeping congressional win

Democrats will take control of both chambers of Congress in January after gaining at least 30 House seats and a Senate majority of at least 54–44. Incoming House leader Hakeem Jeffries said the party will focus on affordability and accountability, while close Senate races in Alaska and Mississippi could widen the majority. The new Congress will have investigative power and the ability to block nominees, but Republicans still control the lame-duck session and a must-pass funding bill. President Trump acknowledged that the Iran war hurt his party, while House Speaker Mike Johnson said he would step aside from leadership next year.

Source: Reuters, November 5

Iran reports two tankers and a tugboat burning in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in separate incidents after the vessels attempted to use unauthorized routes through the Strait of Hormuz. One tanker reportedly struck a mine south of the strait, while a second supertanker caught fire while exiting an eastern route. A tugboat trying to assist the second vessel also burned. The incidents show that maritime risk remains acute even as markets bet that U.S. political pressure could push the Iran conflict toward negotiations.

Source: Reuters, November 5

Business

Oil plunges more than 8% as investors bet on an exit from the Iran war

Brent crude fell 8.7% to $94.62 a barrel and U.S. crude dropped 8.3% to $88.12, their lowest closes since August 31. Traders interpreted the Democratic sweep of Congress and the administration's post-election comments as reducing the odds of a renewed U.S. escalation against Iran. Falling energy prices could ease inflation and borrowing-cost pressure, but negotiations remain fragile and a failed deal could reverse the move quickly. The UAE is separately considering another pipeline that would reduce its dependence on the Strait of Hormuz.

Source: Reuters, November 5

Microsoft declines to expand its Texas data-centre deal with Nscale

Microsoft will not expand a Texas data-centre agreement with British AI infrastructure company Nscale, according to a source familiar with the discussions. A potential extension had reportedly been worth tens of billions of dollars. Nscale is already working to deploy about 200,000 Nvidia chips across U.S. and European facilities and has discussed a separate, very large Anthropic agreement. The decision adds another sign that capital discipline and political resistance are beginning to complicate the AI infrastructure boom.

Source: Reuters, November 5

Tech

Chip and data-centre stocks lag as Washington's AI politics change

Nasdaq futures fell Thursday as chipmakers and data-centre companies remained under pressure after the election. Investors expect a Democratic Congress to scrutinize rapid data-centre construction, electricity costs, water use, and AI regulation more closely. Large platform companies such as Amazon, Meta, and Alphabet have held up better because a slower buildout could reduce their near-term capital burden. Suppliers whose growth assumes an uninterrupted expansion of new capacity face the sharper reset.

Source: Reuters, November 5

AI demand is pulling semiconductor supply away from phones and PCs

Siltronic's chief executive says the AI boom is redirecting chip-manufacturing capacity toward data centres and constraining other end markets. That shift helps explain why strong AI spending can coexist with shortages or slower growth in smartphones, PCs, and other electronics. It also makes the broader economy more dependent on a narrow set of AI infrastructure assumptions. If data-centre projects slow, manufacturers could face a difficult rebalancing after years of capacity and investment decisions.

Source: Reuters, November 4

Nera Watch

California privacy board considers stronger opt-out preference signal rules

The California Privacy Protection Agency board begins a two-day meeting today with proposed amendments to opt-out preference signal rules on the agenda. Staff text would expressly recognize Global Privacy Control as a valid signal, add examples explaining when businesses must honor it, and expand reporting obligations. The board may vote to advance the package into formal rulemaking and a 45-day public-comment period. The proposal is not yet a final rule, but it could materially change consent and data-sharing operations.

Why this is important to Nera: Reliable opt-out propagation is both a compliance requirement and a systems-design problem. Nera can help customers bind consent state and purpose limitations to encrypted computation, proving that opted-out records are not exposed or used while preserving allowed aggregate analysis.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency, November 5

Fortanix brings attested confidential AI workloads to Kubernetes

Fortanix now supports CNCF Confidential Containers in its Confidential AI platform, allowing organizations to run AI workloads on Kubernetes inside hardware-backed trusted execution environments. The integration verifies workload measurements and policy before releasing the keys needed to decrypt models and sensitive data. It supports confidential CPUs and GPUs and uses open-source CoCo workflows to reduce deployment friction. The release pushes hardware-based confidential AI closer to normal enterprise container operations.

Why this is important to Nera: Fortanix is a direct adjacent competitor offering production-oriented protection for data and models while in use. Nera should distinguish FHE's ability to compute without exposing plaintext even inside a trusted enclave, while also evaluating whether enclave-plus-FHE deployments can satisfy customers seeking defense in depth and Kubernetes-native operations.

Source: Fortanix, October 18

California's expanded deletion right takes effect January 1

California's SB 923 closes a loophole by extending the CCPA deletion right to non-exempt personal information regardless of where a business obtained it. The law also strengthens accessible methods for submitting privacy requests and takes effect January 1, 2027. Businesses now have less than two months to map derived, purchased, and indirectly collected data to deletion workflows. The operational challenge will be proving that deletion reaches analytics and AI systems, not merely primary customer databases.

Why this is important to Nera: Broader deletion scope increases the cost of uncontrolled copies and plaintext data pipelines. Nera can position confidential analytics and minimized-data architectures as ways to reduce replication, maintain lineage, and demonstrate that deleted records no longer influence protected queries or model workflows.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency, September 28

Lakers / NBA

Dončić scores 40 as the Lakers beat Sacramento for a third straight win

Luka Dončić scored 40 points with nine rebounds and seven assists in a 116–103 Lakers victory over the Kings. It was his sixth consecutive game with at least 35 points and pushed his total to 308 through his first eight appearances, the most at that stage since Michael Jordan in 1988–89. Austin Reaves added 21 points as Los Angeles improved to 7–2. The Lakers pulled away despite an uneven performance, while Sacramento fell to 1–7.

Source: Associated Press, November 5

The Lakers host the Trail Blazers Friday at 7 p.m. Pacific in West Group C play. The game will stream nationally on Prime Video and gives Los Angeles an immediate tournament test after Wednesday's road win. Dončić's extraordinary scoring has covered stretches of loose execution, but a Cup game raises the cost of turnovers and slow starts because point differential can matter. The home matchup also begins a more demanding stretch than the Kings' record offered.

Source: NBA, November 5

Bay Area

Redwood City's Measure E rent-control initiative continues to trail

Measure E, which would expand rent control and just-cause eviction limits in Redwood City, was receiving about 44% support in the latest reported count. The proposal would apply rent stabilization to many units built before 1995, cap increases, and broaden tenant protections beyond the city's existing ordinance. Results remain unofficial while San Mateo County continues counting ballots, so the margin can still move. The early result suggests voters favored the city's newer tenant-protection framework over a substantially broader replacement.

Source: Redwood City Pulse, November 5

Regional Transit Measure wins a 14-year Bay Area funding mandate

Bay Area voters approved the Regional Transit Measure by a wide margin, creating a 14-year sales tax across five counties. Roughly two-thirds of the money is intended for operations at BART, Muni, Caltrain, AC Transit, ferries, and other agencies, with one-third available for other local transportation priorities. The measure also includes oversight and accountability requirements after a campaign centered on avoiding severe service cuts. Passage gives transit operators time, but it does not eliminate the need to rebuild ridership and control costs.

Source: Metropolitan Transportation Commission, November 4
