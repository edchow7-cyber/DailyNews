Nepal flood toll surges as nearly 3,000 remain missing

Rescue teams resumed operations after glacier-triggered flooding near the Tibet border, with at least 669 people reported dead in Nepal and seven in China. Nearly 3,000 remain missing, including hundreds of foreign nationals, while damaged terrain and unstable lakes complicate the search. Nepal estimates reconstruction at $4 billion to $5 billion and is asking for specialized help with tunnel rescues and DNA identification.

Story 1 of 3

California DROP becomes a high-volume privacy workflow

More than 500,000 Californians have registered for the state’s Delete Request and Opt-out Platform, which sends one deletion request across registered data brokers. Brokers must pull requests at least every 45 days, and California says tens of millions of records have already been deleted. The scale converts privacy rights from an occasional legal request into a recurring operational workload requiring automation and provable execution.

Why it matters to Nera: High-volume privacy workflows strengthen the market for technologies that can match, analyze and act on sensitive data without unnecessarily revealing or centralizing it.

→ CalPrivacy — more than 500,000 Californians have signed up for DROP

Duality and IBM package PETs as sovereign-AI infrastructure

Duality is integrating its privacy-preserving collaboration layer with IBM Sovereign Core so organizations can analyze distributed sensitive data while keeping raw information protected. IBM supplies customer-operated controls for identity, keys, logs and operations, while Duality enables approved analytics and AI across organizational boundaries. The packaging matters because PETs are being sold as part of an enterprise sovereign-AI stack rather than as a standalone cryptography project.

Why it matters to Nera: Duality validates a go-to-market pattern Nera should watch closely: sell the enterprise outcome first and let the privacy technology enable it underneath.

Bay Area ICE arrests up nearly 70%, data project says

A UC Berkeley data project says ICE arrests in the San Francisco area rose from 2,928 in the first seven months of 2025 to 4,970 in the same period this year. The project also reports a large increase in arrests outside jails and prisons, including at check-ins and in the community. DHS disputes the project’s interpretation and methodology, but the figures indicate a substantial change in the scale and visibility of immigration enforcement across the Bay Area.
