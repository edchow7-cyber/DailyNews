Russian strikes force Ukraine’s largest steelmaker offline

ArcelorMittal said repeated Russian strikes have made safe operations impossible at its Kryvyi Rih complex, Ukraine’s largest steel plant. Four attacks in five weeks caused extensive damage and killed five workers, prompting the company to expect a roughly $1 billion impairment charge. The shutdown is another blow to an industry that accounts for about 15% of Ukrainian exports. A separate drone strike in central Kyiv killed a 14-year-old boy as Russia intensified attacks on industrial and civilian infrastructure.

TikTok agrees to teen limits in a landmark state settlement

TikTok and ByteDance settled Alabama’s teen-safety case before it could become the first state lawsuit against the platform to reach a jury. The agreement adds a two-hour daily limit for teen users, pauses after 15 minutes of continuous viewing and tighter age verification. TikTok will pay at least $100 million, with the total potentially reaching $300 million if attorneys general from 40 other states join. The settlement turns a test case over addictive design into a potentially national template for platform controls and enforcement.

California braces for worsening coastal erosion under El Niño

California declared a state of emergency as officials prepare for an El Niño winter that could combine rising sea levels, extreme tides and atmospheric rivers. Summer cyclone swells already opened a sinkhole near Malibu, destroyed a Dana Point home and stripped sand from beaches. Climate researchers warn that naturally occurring cycles are being amplified by climate change, increasing the risk of severe coastal damage. The state is mobilizing disaster relief and first responders before El Niño’s strongest effects arrive later this year.

Saudi Arabia restarts a pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz

Saudi Arabia resumed low-rate operations on its East-West oil pipeline after drone attacks shut it and halted loadings at Yanbu on September 13, sources told Reuters. The route can carry up to 4 million barrels per day—about 4% of global supply—from eastern fields to the Red Sea, bypassing the disrupted Strait of Hormuz. Operators are working toward full capacity while tankers reposition for renewed exports and ship-to-ship transfers. Brent crude fell more than $2 as the restart eased immediate supply concerns.

Fed weighs higher thresholds for stricter bank oversight

The Federal Reserve is preparing a proposal to raise the asset levels that trigger tougher capital, liquidity and stress-testing requirements, sources told Reuters. The highest threshold could move from $700 billion to roughly $960 billion, while some lower-tier requirements could begin closer to $150 billion instead of $100 billion. Banks argue that inflation and economic growth have made the 2019 thresholds outdated. The change could reduce compliance costs for some lenders and encourage consolidation among midsize banks.

Air District reviews Angel Island burn after smoke forecast miss

The Bay Area Air District will review how it approves prescribed burns after smoke from Angel Island spread through San Francisco and western Contra Costa County for the second time this month. The agency acknowledged that actual weather and air-quality conditions diverged from the forecast used to authorize Wednesday’s operation. Crews treated 85 acres before stopping, bringing September’s total to 147 acres, and State Parks says no additional burning is planned for the foreseeable future. Contra Costa Supervisor John Gioia plans to raise the approval process at the air district board’s October 7 meeting.

Story 3 of 4

San Francisco reports a record-low 136 new HIV infections

San Francisco recorded 136 new HIV infections in 2025, twelve fewer than the prior year and a 75% decline over two decades. Prevention medication, rapid connection to treatment and viral suppression have driven the long decline, but city officials say progress remains uneven. Black and Latino residents and transgender women remain disproportionately represented among new cases, while housing and health-care access continue to shape risk. The city’s next challenge is extending prevention and treatment to the communities still least able to reach them.

Failed transit measures could more than double peak Bay Bridge delays

A SPUR-commissioned analysis says deep BART, Muni and Caltrain service cuts could push enough commuters into cars to more than double peak Bay Bridge travel times. The model also projects worsening congestion on Highway 101, Interstate 680 and through the Caldecott Tunnel if November transit-funding measures fail. Bay Area agencies face major deficits as pandemic-era federal aid runs out and ridership remains below its old baseline. Critics call the projections overstated, but the report argues the scale of likely mode switching would create regionwide delays.
