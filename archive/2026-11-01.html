Top Headlines

Interest grows in a limited Black Sea ceasefire

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio says proposals from Turkey and India could protect grain and energy shipments by Russia and Ukraine. Canada is also backing a halt to attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure before winter. Russia has not indicated that it is ready to proceed, and a broader settlement remains distant. Even so, maritime safety and food security may offer a narrower area where diplomacy can still produce practical gains.

Source: Reuters, November 1

France’s third protest weekend draws 320,000 people

The youth-led movement began over deteriorating schools and an admissions system seen as unfair, then expanded into anger about living costs and President Emmanuel Macron. Turnout fell from the previous two weekends but remained nationwide. Most marches were peaceful, though clashes, fires, and more than 560 arrests were reported. Parallel demonstrations across Europe show that education, housing, health, and affordability concerns are converging into a broader generational movement.

Source: Associated Press, October 31

Business

AI spending fears erase roughly $2 trillion from the Nasdaq 100

The index lost more than 5.8% in October as investors questioned whether hyperscalers can turn rapidly rising infrastructure budgets into returns fast enough. Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon all raised spending forecasts, while rising borrowing costs made debt-funded expansion look less forgiving. Revenue remains strong, but the market is distinguishing self-funded growth from capital-intensive promises. The next test is whether Apple and Nvidia can restore confidence without accelerating the same financing concern.

Source: Reuters, October 30

DoorDash’s $17 billion Instacart deal redraws local delivery

DoorDash agreed to pay $65 per Instacart share, a 26% premium to the pre-rumor closing price. The companies plan to combine restaurant, grocery, retail, and merchant technology while retaining Instacart’s brand. The strategic argument is that scale will help local stores compete with the largest commerce platforms. Shareholders and regulators will now test whether those benefits outweigh concentration, merchant-dependence, and integration risks.

Source: DoorDash, October 29

Tech

Military AI is accelerating decisions—and raising the cost of error

The Pentagon is combining intelligence feeds with commercial models to speed analysis and targeting. Officials say the systems synthesize more information, but reported failures include an assessment that nearly prompted a wrongful interdiction and decisions based on stale maps. Faster cycles do not remove civilian-harm, strategic, or dependency risks. Deployment is outpacing agreement on review standards, auditability, and who is accountable when automated analysis fails.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Foxconn prepares another OpenAI infrastructure collaboration

Foxconn says it is developing a new tie-up with OpenAI after Chairman Young Liu met Sam Altman. The relationship would extend earlier work on data-center racks and components. AI servers now contribute more than half of Foxconn’s cloud-and-networking revenue, and the manufacturer expects double-digit growth next year. The expansion shows how the AI buildout is moving deeper into physical manufacturing, power, and supply-chain capacity.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Nera Watch

NIST’s privacy-cryptography workshop shifts FHE toward comparable workloads

WPEC 2026 highlighted a community benchmarking suite covering encrypted vector search, exact arithmetic, transciphering, and machine-learning inference. Its common harness measures time, bandwidth, storage, precision, and cost across a seven-stage protocol. The program reflects a maturing field: end-to-end evidence is becoming more important than isolated primitive speed. Comparable workloads can make procurement and architecture decisions substantially more rigorous.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera should publish workload-level Excel and confidential-AI benchmarks using the same dimensions, making performance claims auditable and competitor comparisons concrete. Alignment with an emerging community harness can reduce buyer uncertainty and accelerate enterprise pilots.

Source: NIST WPEC 2026

NIST’s post-quantum roadmap creates a new enterprise migration clock

NISTIR 8547 identifies vulnerable algorithms, replacement standards, and federal transition milestones. High-value systems face key-establishment targets in 2030 and signature targets in 2031, while 2035 remains the broader federal goal. The report also supports hybrid approaches and emphasizes cryptographic inventory and supply-chain visibility. “Harvest now, decrypt later” makes long-lived confidential data an immediate planning issue rather than a future one.

Why this is important to Nera: FHE protects data during computation, while PQC protects keys and channels against future quantum attacks. Nera needs a crypto-agility story that explains how its confidential-AI architecture will adopt post-quantum key establishment without confusing the two protections.

Source: NIST, October 27

NIST frames confidential computing as a core cloud privacy control

NIST’s draft guidance describes hardware-enabled confidential computing as extending encryption to data while it is actively processed in memory. That helps close the gap between encryption at rest and in transit, especially for sensitive cloud workloads. Hardware enclaves do not remove application, side-channel, or operator risks, but they are becoming part of the enterprise baseline. Buyers will increasingly compare hardware isolation with software and cryptographic approaches such as FHE.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera should explain when FHE provides a stronger trust boundary than enclave-based confidential computing and when a layered design is appropriate. This is both a competitive positioning opportunity and a partner path for workloads that need defense in depth.

Source: NIST IR 8320E

Lakers / NBA

Lakers host Toronto looking to recover from Friday’s collapse

Los Angeles led Golden State by 25 before losing 112–95 and now faces a Raptors team beginning its first West Coast trip. Luka Dončić scored 21 in the first quarter Friday but only 16 afterward, and the Lakers struggled badly when he sat. Toronto brings the league’s third-ranked defense and second-ranked steals attack despite Kawhi Leonard’s absence. The Lakers need a steadier second unit and more force on the glass to avoid another game dictated by momentum swings.

Source: Reuters, November 1

Ball security is tonight’s clearest Lakers pressure point

Los Angeles committed a season-high 20 turnovers against Golden State, including eight by Austin Reaves for a second consecutive game. Toronto is built to punish that weakness with aggressive hands and transition scoring. The Lakers also enter last in rebounds per game, creating another path for the Raptors to manufacture extra possessions. Cleaner initiation and viable non-Luka creation matter more tonight than another explosive individual scoring burst.

Source: Silver Screen and Roll, November 1

Bay Area

Valkyries win the WNBA championship in their second season

Golden State beat Atlanta 68–63 in the league’s first winner-take-all Finals Game 7. Gabby Williams scored 30, delivered the Valkyries’ final 11 points of the third quarter, and earned Finals MVP. Kiah Stokes added 13 rebounds and two blocks as lineup adjustments helped erase a halftime deficit. The two-year-old expansion franchise is the fastest North American major-league expansion team since 1967 to win a championship.

Source: Golden State Valkyries, November 1

Pregnant mother killed and six wounded in Fairfield store shooting

A targeted shooting inside a Spirit Halloween store at Solano Town Center killed 31-year-old Constance Hages and wounded six other people. Police say the incident involved one suspect, and three victims were initially reported in critical condition. Hages was a mother of four, and her family told investigators that she was pregnant. The attack turned a crowded shopping night into a mass-casualty scene hours before Halloween.

Source: Associated Press, October 31
