Federal restrictions sharpen demand for controllable confidential AI

The DeepSeek removal order illustrates how model origin, hidden dependencies, and cross-border data handling can disqualify an otherwise capable AI service. Government buyers need enforceable guarantees about where computation occurs, what leaves the environment, and which components can access prompts or outputs. Those questions will spread into regulated commercial procurement as model supply chains grow more complex.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera can turn confidential inference, verifiable deployment boundaries, and model/data lineage into concrete procurement controls. The opportunity is to help customers use powerful models without exposing sensitive inputs or losing visibility into the serving stack; the threat is that opaque dependencies can block deals even when cryptography is strong.

Agent-driven attacks increase the value of encrypted computation

The reported campaign against more than 1,200 websites shows how inexpensive agents can compress discovery, exploitation, and persistence into a repeatable workflow. Traditional controls are vulnerable when an attacker can continuously search for weak identities, exposed endpoints, and forgotten integrations. Protecting data only at rest or in transit is insufficient when AI systems actively process sensitive material in memory.

Why this is important to Nera: This is a direct demand signal for Nera Systems' confidential-AI and PET positioning. FHE and other protected-compute approaches can reduce plaintext exposure during inference, while policy-bound tool access can constrain agents; Nera must also prove its own orchestration and key-management layers withstand autonomous adversarial testing.

China's proposed export controls reshape model-market access

China has proposed controls on 23 AI technologies within a broader revision of its technology-export catalogue. The framework could restrict overseas model deployments while leaving some API and software-service access available under tighter oversight. The distinction between exporting a model and providing remote inference will matter to cloud vendors, developers, and customers building global AI products.

Why this is important to Nera: Export rules create both opportunity and compliance risk. Nera can offer jurisdiction-aware confidential inference that keeps data and model assets within approved boundaries, but it must map cryptographic deployments to export-control obligations and avoid presenting PETs as a way to bypass licensing or national-security restrictions.
