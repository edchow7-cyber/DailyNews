Top Headlines

Israel’s election leaves no clear path to a governing coalition

Israel’s election produced an opposition majority but no obvious coalition capable of immediately replacing caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Once final results are published, President Isaac Herzog will consult the parties and choose the person most likely to form a government. The process can run roughly 100 days, cycle through multiple candidates, and end in another election if every coalition attempt fails. The uncertainty is already testing markets and the country’s political alliances.

Source: Reuters, October 29

Localized truce holds as humanitarian aid prepares to enter Oleshky

A localized Russia–Ukraine ceasefire around Oleshky reached a third day without a reported violation. Teams were clearing mines from access roads so food, medicine, and evacuations could begin Friday. The operation remains narrow and fighting continues elsewhere, but sustained access would give civilians in the occupied city their first meaningful relief. Its durability may also offer a small test case for broader U.S.-led diplomacy.

Source: Associated Press, October 29

U.S. economy grows at a 3.4% annual rate

Third-quarter growth accelerated to a 3.4% annual rate, ahead of forecasts and up from 2.2% in the prior quarter. Consumer spending rose 3.8%, while business investment was boosted by a sharp increase in information-processing equipment tied to the AI buildout. The report points to resilient demand despite elevated borrowing costs. It also makes the Federal Reserve’s next inflation-and-rate decision more complicated.

Source: Associated Press, October 29

Business

Fed pause and Big Tech earnings redraw the market’s fault lines

The Federal Reserve held rates steady while investors parsed a fresh wave of technology earnings. Treasury yields and equity leadership now reflect two competing ideas: the economy is still strong, but the cost of funding the AI boom is rising. Companies able to show durable cash generation are separating from those asking investors to wait for future returns. That tension is likely to shape capital allocation through year-end.

Source: Reuters, October 29

Wall Street demands cash-flow discipline from the AI spending boom

Microsoft and Oracle gained favor after showing clearer paths from infrastructure spending to revenue and cash flow. Alphabet fell sharply as capital expenditures consumed more than its operating cash flow even while Google Cloud more than doubled revenue. Microsoft generated $18.6 billion in free cash flow and guided to faster Azure growth. Investors are no longer rewarding AI spending equally; they want evidence that scale can finance itself.

Source: Reuters, October 29

Consumer confidence improves, but households remain selective

Fresh business indicators suggest consumers feel better than they did earlier in the fall, even as affordability concerns remain entrenched. Strong spending helped power the third-quarter expansion, showing that households have not broadly retreated. The mix still favors value, reliability, and essential purchases over discretionary excess. Retailers entering the holiday season will need to convert optimism into traffic without assuming price sensitivity has disappeared.

Source: Associated Press business briefing, October 29

Tech

Google argues homomorphic encryption is becoming practical for AI

Google is expanding its Private Computing Toolkit with HEIR, infrastructure designed to make homomorphic-encryption development easier and faster. The approach lets systems compute on encrypted data without first exposing the underlying plaintext. Tooling and compiler maturity, not just cryptographic theory, will determine whether private AI reaches mainstream developers. Google’s entry is a signal that encrypted inference is moving closer to a platform capability.

Source: Google Security Blog

NVIDIA’s confidential-computing ecosystem expands around AI infrastructure

NVIDIA says cloud and model providers are using hardware-backed confidential computing to protect customer data and model intellectual property during execution. The ecosystem pitch combines accelerated inference with attestation and isolated memory. It does not eliminate every trust dependency, but it gives enterprises a deployable control while cryptographic computation matures. The market is beginning to expect confidential execution as an infrastructure option rather than a niche feature.

Source: NVIDIA, October 21

Frontier-AI financing faces a harder test

The market’s new focus on cash generation is raising the bar for frontier-model companies and their backers. Enormous training and inference costs can create strategic leverage, but they can also lock companies into repeated capital raises and infrastructure dependencies. Enterprise safeguards and predictable commercial demand are becoming as important as benchmark gains. The next phase of competition will reward firms that can prove both technical differentiation and durable unit economics.

Source: Anthropic, September 1

Nera Watch

NIST publishes a concrete transition path to post-quantum cryptography

NIST’s final IR 8547 gives organizations a structured roadmap for migrating systems away from quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography. The guidance turns “crypto agility” into a planning, inventory, dependency, and procurement problem that enterprises must address now. Long-lived data and embedded systems make delayed migration especially risky. The roadmap will influence customer security questionnaires and regulated-sector purchasing.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera can position confidential AI and PET deployments inside a broader cryptographic-modernization program, pairing data-in-use protection with quantum-safe key exchange and signatures. That creates consultative opportunities with customers that need a staged migration rather than a single product swap.

Source: NIST, October 27

NIST’s privacy-enhancing cryptography workshop shows the field converging

WPEC 2026 brought together work on fully homomorphic encryption, private set intersection, zero-knowledge proofs, and privacy-preserving digital credentials. The agenda reflects a shift from isolated cryptographic primitives toward systems that combine techniques around real workflows. Interoperability, benchmarking, and deployment evidence remain central barriers. Customers will increasingly compare PET platforms on integration and operational proof, not theory alone.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera’s differentiation should emphasize orchestration across FHE and adjacent PETs, measurable performance, and deployment-ready confidential-AI workflows. The workshop also provides a map of standards stakeholders, potential partners, and likely enterprise evaluation criteria.

Source: NIST WPEC 2026, October 26–28

Locktera joins NVIDIA Inception and pushes confidential RAG

Locktera says its confidential-AI stack combines persistent data controls with encrypted retrieval and generation workflows. Joining NVIDIA Inception gives the company access to technical resources, ecosystem credibility, and business-development support. The announcement is an early but direct signal of how competitors are packaging “confidential RAG” for enterprise buyers. The NVIDIA relationship could shorten its route to customers already standardized on accelerated infrastructure.

Why this is important to Nera: Locktera is a relevant competitor in confidential AI and encrypted-data processing. Nera should compare its own FHE depth, trust model, performance evidence, and integration story against Locktera’s persistent-control narrative while watching for channel or customer overlap in regulated industries.

Source: Locktera, October 7

Bay Area

High-tide flooding keeps parts of the Bay Area under advisories

Elevated tides are producing road closures and localized flooding along low-lying portions of the Northern California coast and bay shoreline. A coastal flood advisory remains in effect as the highest water levels arrive around the morning tide cycle. Drivers should expect intermittent closures near the Embarcadero, Richardson Bay, and other flood-prone approaches. The event is another reminder that routine infrastructure disruptions are arriving without a major storm.

Source: ABC7 Bay Area, October 29

Federal judge rejects request to segregate 20,000 California ballots

A federal judge denied the Justice Department’s emergency request to set aside roughly 20,000 ballots associated with people the administration described as likely noncitizens. State and county officials argued that the late operational change would disrupt voting and risk affecting eligible voters. The opinion framed the request against a long history of clashes over ballot access. The broader litigation remains politically significant with Election Day approaching.

Source: ABC7 Bay Area, October 29

Valkyries can win their first championship today

Golden State leads Atlanta 3–2 in the WNBA Finals and can close the series in Game 6. The Valkyries’ defense held the Dream to 55 points and 29.2% shooting in Game 5, giving the second-year franchise a title opportunity. The unusual 9:30 a.m. Pacific start has drawn frustration, but the basketball stakes are simple: one more defensive performance brings a championship to the Bay. The game is at State Farm Arena in Atlanta.

Source: Golden State Valkyries, October 28
