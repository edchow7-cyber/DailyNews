House prepares a sweeping Russia-sanctions vote

The U.S. House plans to take up a Russia sanctions bill next week after the Senate passed it 86–11. The measure would tighten pressure on Moscow and authorize steep tariffs against countries that continue buying Russian energy. Its movement is notable because the bill was championed by the late Senator Lindsey Graham and now has bipartisan momentum in both chambers. If enacted, the secondary-tariff provisions could reshape energy trade well beyond Russia and add another source of price pressure to already strained oil markets.

Modi and Putin deepen ties ahead of the BRICS summit

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Russian President Vladimir Putin used talks in New Delhi to reaffirm their strategic partnership and pursue a $100 billion trade target by 2030. Defense and energy remain central even as India tries to balance its Russian ties with growing relationships in the West. The meeting comes just before the BRICS summit, giving both leaders a platform to demonstrate that Moscow retains major partners despite Western sanctions. For Washington and Europe, the durable India–Russia channel complicates efforts to isolate the Kremlin economically.

Oil eases but closes a punishing week above $100

Oil pulled back Friday on tentative diplomatic signals, with Brent around $104.53 and WTI near $100.25, but both benchmarks were still headed for weekly gains above 8%. The supply threat has moved beyond crude: U.S. diesel prices reached a record above $6 a gallon as Gulf disruption and attacks on Russian refineries tightened refined products. Analysts continue to see substantial upside risk even if talks temporarily calm the market. The combination of expensive fuel and fragile shipping is the clearest near-term channel from geopolitics into household inflation and interest rates.

Tanker rates hit records as Gulf shipping risk compounds

The cost of chartering a very large crude carrier from the Gulf of Oman to China reached a record Worldscale 450, equal to roughly $11.50 per barrel. Rates surged after attacks on ships near Hormuz and the Houthi advance toward the Bab el-Mandeb tightened vessel availability and insurance appetite. The shock is spreading to West Africa–Asia routes, showing that the freight penalty is no longer confined to the Gulf. Even if the crude price stabilizes, record transport costs can keep energy and goods inflation elevated.

A lawyer is fined after ChatGPT invents witnesses

New Mexico’s Supreme Court fined defense lawyer Stephen Aarons $5,000 and held him in contempt after an appellate brief included fabricated police testimony and witnesses. Aarons said he used ChatGPT to summarize trial proceedings and failed to verify the result. The appeal concerns a client serving a life sentence, making the lapse more than a routine citation error. The case is a sharp reminder that professional AI workflows need source-grounded review and accountability, not merely a warning label about hallucinations.

Story 2 of 2

California enacts a sweeping child-tech package

Governor Gavin Newsom signed 13 bills aimed at reducing digital harms to minors. The package restricts addictive features such as infinite scroll and autoplay for users under 16, pauses sales of AI-chatbot toys for four years, and expands criminal penalties involving AI-generated child sexual-abuse material. It also adds parental-control and safety-assessment requirements for chatbot products. The laws are among the broadest state efforts yet to regulate both social platforms and consumer AI around children, though civil-liberties groups argue portions may be overly restrictive.

Story 1 of 2

Claude abuse maps the widening AI threat surface

Anthropic says actors attempted to use Claude for biological-weapons research, vulnerability discovery, government-style surveillance, cyber operations, and industrialized romance fraud. One campaign targeted 42 European entities; another used thousands of AI personas across more than 20 dating apps. Many operators bypassed geographic restrictions, which underscores the limits of account-level controls once sensitive models are broadly reachable. The incidents show how identity, intent, data access, and model behavior have to be governed together.

Why it matters to Nera: Buyers need enforcement around the computation itself—who can combine which data with which model and what derived information can leave—not just perimeter access and encrypted storage.

Story 2 of 2

Finance-specific AI raises the bar for enforceable data boundaries

OpenAI launched a financial-services version of ChatGPT built for investment banking and equity research, with integrations spanning market data, private-company information, financial models, and firm-specific templates. The product emphasizes role-based access, encryption, compliance logs, and connections to existing data subscriptions. Those controls are important, but financial workflows often combine material nonpublic information, licensed datasets, and internal analysis during the same computation. As specialized agents move from summarization into modeling and client deliverables, firms will need technical guarantees about which inputs can be combined, what each user may learn, and how derived outputs are exposed.

Why it matters to Nera: This is a direct market signal for confidential computation in FP&A, banking, and private equity—the same buyers Nera is targeting—where permissions and encryption must remain meaningful while sensitive data is actively processed.

→ Reuters — ChatGPT for financial services

Kevon Looney’s value rests on the small things

Kevon Looney enters the Lakers’ season as the dependable backup to Walker Kessler rather than a headline addition. His screening, offensive rebounding, positioning, and willingness to do low-usage work should help stabilize bench units built around Luka Dončić’s playmaking. The risk is whether declining foot speed and finishing can survive against faster lineups. If Looney remains physically sound, Los Angeles may have bought exactly the kind of connective role player its more glamorous roster pieces need.

BART restores the Green Line but repair work continues

BART says all stations are open and Green Line service has been restored after the Daly City equipment fire, but riders should still expect five-to-20-minute delays around Daly City and Colma. Crews are continuing overnight repairs, and some line patterns remain altered while damaged power and train-control equipment is replaced. The update is a meaningful improvement from the morning’s suspension, though it does not yet represent a full return to normal. Weekend riders should also note planned bus bridges between Union City and Warm Springs on September 12–13.

Story 2 of 2

The 49ers rout the Rams in the NFL’s first Australia game

San Francisco beat the favored Rams 27–7 before more than 100,000 fans at the Melbourne Cricket Ground. Brock Purdy threw three touchdown passes, the defense held Matthew Stafford to 155 passing yards, and a fourth-and-goal stop set up a decisive 99-yard scoring drive. The 49ers’ decision to arrive a week early appeared to pay off against a Rams team that landed only about 28 hours before kickoff. It is an unusually strong opening statement for a healthier San Francisco roster—and a striking example of preparation becoming a competitive advantage.
