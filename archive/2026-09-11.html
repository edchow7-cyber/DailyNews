Russia and Ukraine trade deadly overnight drone strikes

Ukrainian drone strikes killed two people and injured three in Russia’s Tula region, while Russian attacks injured eight in Kyiv and set fire to a nine-story apartment building. Additional strikes damaged industrial sites and residential areas in Saratov and Volgograd, including a logistics hub and reported refinery targets. Kyiv describes the campaign as an effort to bring the cost of war deeper into Russia; both governments continue to deny deliberately targeting civilians. The exchange shows the conflict’s air war expanding again after a brief diplomatic lull.

Hong Kong’s IPO rebound begins pulling finance talent back

Hong Kong raised $83.5 billion through IPOs in the first eight months of 2026, up 76% from a year earlier, according to Reuters. The renewed deal flow is attracting professionals back from Singapore, London, Dubai, and mainland China, particularly in wealth management, compliance, risk, and AI integration. More than 400 companies have expanded or established operations in the city this year, while demand for top-tier Central office space has strengthened. The rebound does not erase Hong Kong’s political and cost-of-living challenges, but it does mark a meaningful recovery in its role as a China-linked global capital hub.

California enacts a sweeping child-tech package

Governor Gavin Newsom signed 13 bills aimed at reducing digital harms to minors. The package restricts addictive features such as infinite scroll and autoplay for users under 16, pauses sales of AI-chatbot toys for four years, and expands criminal penalties involving AI-generated child sexual-abuse material. It also adds parental-control and safety-assessment requirements for chatbot products. The laws are among the broadest state efforts yet to regulate both social platforms and consumer AI around children, though civil-liberties groups argue portions may be overly restrictive.

→ Reuters — California child-tech laws

Anthropic says it disrupted state-backed model abuse and extraction

Anthropic says Russian and Chinese actors used Claude in campaigns involving phishing, malware evasion, intelligence work, and attempts to reproduce model capabilities. The company alleges that seven China-based labs used illicit distillation or live-data rerouting, with the largest campaign exceeding 151 million interactions. The report also describes criminals using models for cyber operations and weapons-related software. The scale matters because frontier-model security now includes not only preventing harmful answers, but detecting industrialized extraction, identity abuse, and automated misuse across millions of seemingly ordinary requests.

→ Reuters — Anthropic threat report

Story 1 of 2

Model extraction turns confidential AI into a board-level control issue

Anthropic’s account of large-scale distillation attacks shows that proprietary AI can leak through legitimate-looking interfaces even when model weights and training data never leave the provider. Enterprises face the same pattern with internal copilots and specialized models: repeated queries can expose sensitive logic, derived information, and valuable behavior. Encryption at rest and in transit cannot address leakage that occurs while the system is actively computing. The control plane therefore needs identity, query policy, rate-pattern detection, and ways to minimize what any model or caller can see during execution.

Why it matters to Nera: This strengthens Nera’s case for protecting computation and derived results—not only raw inputs—and gives finance, healthcare, and IP-heavy buyers a concrete model-security story.

→ Reuters — Anthropic disrupts extraction campaigns

Story 2 of 2

California’s rules move AI governance closer to runtime enforcement

California’s new child-safety package pairs product restrictions with safety assessments and parental controls, extending governance beyond disclosures and privacy policies. As AI reaches toys, chatbots, and other interactive products, regulators are increasingly focused on what systems may do for particular users in real time. That creates pressure for enforceable age, purpose, and data-use boundaries at the point of computation. It also signals that sensitive AI deployments will need auditable technical controls, not merely contractual promises.

Why it matters to Nera: Nera can frame confidential policy enforcement as infrastructure that lets enterprises compute only the permitted result for a governed user or workflow while reducing exposure of the underlying data.

→ Reuters — California child-tech package

BART blames heat and aging equipment for a third day of disruption

BART apologized after three days of major disruptions linked to extreme heat and aging infrastructure. A failed HVAC unit in a roughly 40-year-old Daly City traction-power substation contributed to a fire that damaged train-control equipment and a power cable. Most lines returned to normal schedules, but Green Line service remained suspended and Peninsula trains continued to run at reduced speeds while crews worked overnight. For riders heading to SFO or traveling along the Peninsula, the practical message is that service is operating but still fragile, with no firm timeline for full restoration.

Story 2 of 2

The 49ers rout the Rams in the NFL’s first Australia game

San Francisco beat the favored Rams 27–7 before more than 100,000 fans at the Melbourne Cricket Ground. Brock Purdy threw three touchdown passes, the defense held Matthew Stafford to 155 passing yards, and a fourth-and-goal stop set up a decisive 99-yard scoring drive. The 49ers’ decision to arrive a week early appeared to pay off against a Rams team that landed only about 28 hours before kickoff. It is an unusually strong opening statement for a healthier San Francisco roster—and a striking example of preparation becoming a competitive advantage.

→ Associated Press — 49ers beat Rams in Melbourne
