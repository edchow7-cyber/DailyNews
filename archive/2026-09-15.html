Saudi pipeline outage lifts oil nearly 2%

Oil prices rose nearly 2% Tuesday as Saudi Arabia’s East–West pipeline remained offline after attacks on energy infrastructure. Brent traded around $107.55 and U.S. crude around $103.27, while commodity-vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz fell to just four ships Monday. The pipeline is a critical bypass when Gulf shipping is disrupted, so its continued outage removes an important pressure valve. The combination keeps inflation and supply-security risks elevated.

China puts technology-security exit restrictions into effect

China’s new entry-and-exit rules took effect Tuesday, allowing authorities to block citizens from leaving if they are considered a risk to national technology security. Travel restrictions already applied to some officials and people connected to sensitive work, but the new framework explicitly ties mobility to technology and national-security concerns. The measure adds another tool to Beijing’s effort to protect strategic know-how amid intensifying competition with the United States. It also creates fresh compliance and personnel risks for companies with technical staff in China.

Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority issued an updated list of 77 vessels it says violated passage protocols in the Strait of Hormuz. Tracking data showed commodity-vessel transits dropping sharply, though ships operating without AIS may not be counted. The policy adds legal and insurance uncertainty to an already disrupted global energy route.

Story 5 of 5

Hormuz disruption pushes Asian LNG users toward coal and oil

Industry executives expect China and India’s LNG imports to recover only after the Middle East supply crunch ends and prices ease. The conflict has prevented Qatar and the United Arab Emirates from exporting most LNG through the Strait of Hormuz, where roughly one-fifth of global supply previously passed. Price-sensitive buyers have responded by switching some power generation to coal and oil. The shift shows how the conflict is changing fuel demand well beyond crude markets.

→ Reuters — Asian LNG demand and the Middle East supply crunch

Chipmakers commit to ASML’s next-generation lithography

Samsung and SK Hynix plan production use of ASML’s High NA lithography systems in 2028, TSMC has committed for 2030 and Intel says it has already processed more than one million wafers. The geopolitical AI race may be too large to slow

A Reuters analysis argues that frontier AI is now tied so closely to national security and geopolitical competition that safety warnings may accelerate the race rather than pause it. Governments fear that falling behind could be more dangerous than moving quickly, especially if advanced systems become strategic infrastructure. That dynamic could bring much heavier state direction and surveillance of the industry. The central policy problem is therefore coordination among rivals, not simply company-level restraint.

South Korea prepares security rules for autonomous AI agents

South Korea’s state-run internet security agency is developing updated guidelines for AI agents that can act with limited human oversight. The work reflects a shift from passive model risks toward systems that can browse, communicate and execute tasks across services. Security guidance will need to address authorization, identity, auditability and what happens when an agent exceeds its intended scope. The initiative is an early regulatory signal that autonomous-agent controls are becoming a distinct policy category.

Why it matters to Nera: This development raises the value of verifiable privacy, access controls, minimized data exposure and auditable governance in deployed AI systems.

Federal judge blocks construction of an ICE center near Gilroy

A federal judge indefinitely blocked construction of a proposed ICE detention center near Gilroy after finding that the federal government had not completed the required environmental review. The ruling cited hazardous-soil concerns at the former pesticide-research site and questions about whether its septic system could support a large occupancy increase. Santa Clara County sought the injunction, arguing that construction began without adequate transparency or permits. The court will define the precise scope of allowed activity by September 30.

West Oakland BART access changes accompany housing construction

Construction has begun on 240 affordable homes at West Oakland BART, cutting daily station parking from about 440 spaces to 200. Bus stops, passenger pickup, bicycle parking and circulation routes are also moving while accessible pickup remains in front of the station. Vehicle, pedestrian and bicycle access stays open on the east and north sides, with southern access restricted. The larger transit-oriented plan calls for up to 762 homes plus retail, offices and redesigned streets.
