Top Headlines

Cost of living dominates as Americans vote for control of Congress

Americans vote Tuesday in midterm elections that will determine control of Congress for the final two years of President Trump's term. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found the rising cost of living at the center of voters' concerns, while the president's approval has fallen from earlier in his second term. The Justice Department planned a much larger election-monitor deployment than in the previous midterms, drawing concern from some local officials and reassurance from the department. The result will shape legislative power, oversight, and the administration's room to act through 2028.

Source: Reuters, November 3

U.S. intelligence sharing has slowed across government and with Congress

Current and former officials told Reuters that intelligence sharing on Iran and other global threats has slowed and sometimes stopped during the Trump administration's second term. They described tensions among the White House, intelligence agencies, and Congress, including a halt to congressional briefings on election threats. The disruption comes as the administration weighs further action involving Iran after the midterms. Weak information flow can impair both operational decisions and legislative oversight during a volatile security period.

Source: Reuters, November 3

Business

Financial leaders say the AI buildout needs far more capital—and carries growing risk

Technology companies will need substantial new financing to meet demand for AI compute, data centers, and power, according to executives gathered at a major financial conference. S&P expects six hyperscalers to spend more than $7 trillion on AI-related infrastructure from 2025 through 2030, with negative free cash flow extending at least through next year. Blackstone and major banks said demand still exceeds supply, but warned that expensive capital and overly optimistic revenue assumptions could trigger volatility. The central business question is no longer whether AI investment continues, but which projects can justify their financing.

Source: Reuters, November 3

Investors prepare for a possible shift in congressional control

Markets entered Election Day focused on whether a Democratic gain in Congress could change spending, regulation, and scrutiny of the AI sector. Investors told Reuters that the vote had weighed on sentiment for weeks, especially alongside debt-ceiling concerns and unease about data-center spending. Historical patterns show broad U.S. equities often strengthen in the six months after midterms, but that record does not remove near-term policy risk. A divided government could constrain major legislation while increasing investigations and deadline-driven fiscal volatility.

Source: Reuters, November 3

Tech

Schwarz Digits and Bull plan AI computing under European jurisdiction

French supercomputer maker Bull and Schwarz Digits, the technology arm of Lidl owner Schwarz Group, signed a preliminary agreement to build AI computing services operating entirely under European jurisdiction. Schwarz's STACKIT cloud would deliver the service while Bull manufactures the equipment, and the partners may eventually add SAP business software. The project responds to demand from companies and public bodies for AI infrastructure with stronger regional control over data and operations. It also shows sovereignty becoming a core procurement criterion alongside performance and price.

Source: Reuters, November 3

White House refuses to disclose its process for reviewing frontier AI security risks

The White House declined to release records describing a new process for assessing AI tools before public release, citing national-security exemptions. A watchdog group sued after officials identified relevant documents but withheld all of them, including seven pages describing the review system. The administration argues that secrecy protects sensitive capabilities; critics say an important regulatory mechanism is being built without public accountability. The dispute will test how transparency law applies when AI oversight intersects classified cyber and national-security analysis.

Source: The Washington Post, November 2

Nera Watch

FTC orders nine major companies to explain personalized pricing and compensation

The Federal Trade Commission ordered airlines, ride-share platforms, delivery companies, and retailers to disclose how they use personal data to vary prices or worker compensation. The inquiry asks about inputs such as browsing behavior, loyalty participation, phone use, and prior offer acceptance. Companies have 45 days to respond, giving the agency a direct view into systems that may infer willingness to pay or work. The investigation moves personalized pricing from abstract privacy debate toward operational evidence and potential enforcement.

Why this is important to Nera: Pricing analytics are commercially valuable but legally sensitive because raw behavioral data can reveal far more than the decision requires. Nera can position confidential computation and FHE as a way to score permitted attributes without exposing underlying customer or worker records, while producing auditable proof of data minimization for regulators and enterprise buyers.

Source: Federal Trade Commission, October 29

Colorado's automated-decision and chatbot rules approach a January 1 start

Colorado's Automated Decision-Making Technology Act and Chatbot Safety Act take effect January 1, 2027, after an extensive rulemaking process. Proposed rules address developer documentation, deployer disclosures, consumer correction rights, meaningful human review, age assurance, and safeguards for minors. Recent stakeholder comments have focused on interoperability with other state regimes and on aligning developer information with deployer obligations. Companies serving Colorado now have weeks—not quarters—to map systems, data flows, notices, and review procedures.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera's customers will need privacy controls that survive across developer and deployer boundaries, especially in consequential decisions. Productizing encrypted inference, purpose-limited data access, and reviewable decision logs can turn compliance pressure into a near-term sales opportunity for regulated enterprises and AI vendors.

Source: Wiley, November 2

Locktera joins NVIDIA Inception with FHE-secured retrieval for enterprise AI

Locktera says its Confidential RAG product computes vector-similarity scores directly on encrypted data, returning encrypted results to an authorized client for ranking. The company reports internal benchmarks over datasets of up to one million vectors with execution measured in seconds and plans to use NVIDIA Inception resources to improve GPU acceleration and commercialization. The design targets a crucial enterprise-AI problem: searching sensitive embeddings without exposing plaintext to the retrieval infrastructure. Locktera's progress is an early competitive signal that confidential RAG is moving from research toward productized deployment.

Why this is important to Nera: Locktera is a direct adjacent competitor in FHE-powered confidential AI and may shape buyer expectations around encrypted vector search, latency, and GPU support. Nera should benchmark its own retrieval stack against these claims, clarify differentiation across the full AI workflow, and pursue design partners before secure RAG becomes a crowded category.

Source: Locktera, October 7

Lakers / NBA

Dončić scores 42 as the Lakers roll past Miami

Luka Dončić scored 42 points with 13 assists as Los Angeles beat Miami 138–117 and improved to 6–2. Austin Reaves added 27 points, while Walker Kessler posted 15 points and 15 rebounds. The Lakers shot 57.3% from the field and opened the third quarter with an 11–0 run that turned a manageable halftime lead into control of the game. Dončić has now topped 40 points four times in eight games, giving Los Angeles one of the league's hottest early-season offenses.

Source: Reuters, November 3

Sacramento is next as the Lakers try to carry their offense onto the road

The Lakers visit the Kings on Wednesday at 7 p.m. Pacific after sweeping a home back-to-back against Toronto and Miami. Los Angeles is 6–2 and has repeatedly survived turnover trouble through elite shot-making, rebounding from Kessler, and Dončić's extraordinary scoring. Sacramento will test whether that offense travels and whether the Lakers can clean up possessions on one day of rest. Friday's NBA Cup home game against Portland follows, making the Kings matchup the start of a meaningful early-November sequence.

Source: Silver Screen and Roll, November 3

Bay Area

Santa Clara County finishes Election Day preparations as voters make a final push

Registrar staff and volunteers worked late Monday collecting and processing ballots from vote centers across Santa Clara County. Early voting has been strong, but counting will continue for days after polls close because mail and provisional ballots require verification. Voters can still cast a ballot in person or use an official drop box until 8 p.m. Tuesday. Local officials urged residents to verify locations and use official county channels for results and status updates.

Source: NBC Bay Area, November 3

Two rounds of rain arrive for a chilly Bay Area Election Day

A Pacific low is expected to bring a steady band of morning rain followed by scattered heavier showers and possible thunderstorms through the evening commute. Temperatures will remain below normal, generally in the upper 50s to mid-60s. The timing could complicate travel to polling places and ballot drop boxes, especially during commute periods. Conditions should turn drier and milder Wednesday, improving the outlook for San Francisco's Valkyries championship parade.

Source: San Francisco Chronicle, November 3
