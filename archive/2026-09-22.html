Typhoon Dujuan kills four as landslides and flooding hit eastern Japan

Torrential rain from Typhoon Dujuan triggered landslides and flooding along Japan’s Pacific coast, killing four people and leaving six missing, according to NHK. Kanagawa and Chiba were among the hardest-hit areas, with homes flooded or buried and rescue crews searching in Miura. About 45,000 households lost power as rivers overflowed and transport was disrupted. The casualty update materially advances Monday’s warning-stage story.

Houthi forces press toward strategic highlands in southern Yemen

Houthi forces have captured much of Yemen’s Red Sea coast and are advancing toward the Kahboub Mountains in Taiz and Lahij provinces. Control of the highlands could sever links between coastal areas and Saudi-backed government forces while strengthening Houthi influence near the Bab al-Mandab shipping route. The renewed fighting has killed nearly 700 people and displaced more than 120,000, according to reporting cited by Reuters. Diplomatic efforts remain stalled as the conflict adds pressure to regional energy and shipping networks.

Pakistan said airstrikes along the Afghanistan border killed 28 militants in Paktika and Kunar provinces. Story 4 of 5

World Bank and IMF approve a broader debt-risk framework for poorer countries

The World Bank and International Monetary Fund approved the first major overhaul of their joint debt-sustainability framework for low-income countries since 2017. The revised approach adds stronger analysis of domestic debt, longer-term development pressures and more demanding stress tests. It also calls for better debt-data transparency as poorer countries rely more heavily on commercial borrowing. The institutions expect to implement the changes in the second half of 2027.

→ Reuters — World Bank and IMF approve debt-framework changes

UN warns another 100,000 Yemenis may be displaced as fighting grows

More than 130,000 people have already fled their homes in Yemen this month as Houthi forces advance against the Saudi-backed government, the UN refugee agency said. UNHCR expects another 100,000 people could be displaced within three months if the fighting continues. More than 3,000 people have crossed the Red Sea toward Djibouti and Somalia, with that number potentially reaching 10,000 as sailing conditions improve. The agency’s Yemen response plan remains less than 20% funded.

Saudi Arabia restarts a pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz

Saudi Arabia resumed low-rate operations on its East-West oil pipeline after drone attacks shut it and halted loadings at Yanbu on September 13, sources told Reuters. The route can carry up to 4 million barrels per day—about 4% of global supply—from eastern fields to the Red Sea, bypassing the disrupted Strait of Hormuz. Operators are working toward full capacity while tankers reposition for renewed exports and ship-to-ship transfers. Brent crude fell more than $2 as the restart eased immediate supply concerns.

EU proposes energy and water disclosure labels for data centers

The European Commission proposed rules requiring large data centers to disclose standardized measures of energy and water efficiency. Facilities above 500 kilowatts would also report how their water use relates to local stress and whether they can reuse waste heat or support the surrounding power grid. The proposal does not cap consumption, but the label could become a foundation for mandatory minimum standards. Europe expects data-center capacity to more than double by 2030 as it expands AI infrastructure.

→ Reuters — EU proposes data-center energy and water labels

Story 2 of 4

ECB opens a central-bank-money bridge to blockchain markets

The European Central Bank launched Pontes, a service that connects its payment system to blockchain-based financial markets. Participating institutions can settle tokenized transactions in ECB-backed euros instead of private stablecoins. Deutsche Bank, Santander and Clearstream are among the early participants, while the ECB also plans limited investment in highly rated euro-denominated blockchain securities. The project is part of a broader central-bank effort to modernize settlement without giving up sovereign-money controls.

→ Reuters — ECB opens blockchain link to financial markets

Ireland fines Google €403 million over location-data processing

Ireland’s Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million after finding GDPR violations in three location-data features. The inquiry covered Web & App Activity, Location History and Location Accuracy practices from 2018 through 2020. Regulators said the processing reduced users’ control over personal information and retained location data longer than necessary. Google was ordered to bring the affected processing into compliance within six months.

Spain says frontier AI cannot be left to industry self-regulation

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said Monday that companies controlling advanced AI should not be responsible for regulating themselves. Spain plans a 12-month roadmap combining stronger cybersecurity defenses with an AI gigafactory and public-interest models for climate, health and energy. Story 4 of 5

Banks warn AI shopping agents could amplify fraud and privacy risks

A coalition including Bank of America, Capital One, NatWest, ING and Commonwealth Bank of Australia warned that AI shopping agents are advancing faster than consumer-protection standards. Their report highlights scams, mistaken purchases, exposure of payment credentials and agents steering customers toward weaker payment protections. The banks called for disclosure when AI participates in a transaction, stronger data safeguards, explainable choices and interoperability between services. For privacy-enhancing technology vendors, the report points directly to demand for controlled data sharing, auditable agent actions and minimized payment-data exposure.

Marin wildfire sends smoke across four Bay Area counties

The Chileno Fire burned roughly 341 acres west of Petaluma on Sunday and prompted an air-quality advisory across Marin, Sonoma, Napa and Solano counties. Firefighters halted its forward progress Sunday evening, though containment remained limited overnight. New trail will bypass a hazardous Golden Gate cycling route

A 1,600-foot paved trail near the Golden Gate Bridge is expected to open by the end of September, giving northbound cyclists a safer alternative to a dangerous section of Alexander Avenue. The 15-foot-wide Vista Point Trail will include separate space for cyclists and pedestrians as it passes through Fort Baker. At least 83 cyclists were injured on the existing route between 2011 and 2020, according to data cited by the Chronicle. A ribbon-cutting is scheduled for September 30, though the trail could open earlier.
