New allegations focus attention on Zaporizhzhia’s emergency fuel

The head of Russia’s state nuclear corporation accused Ukraine of striking diesel-fuel trucks near the Russian-held Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant, killing two Russian servicemen; Ukraine had not responded in the Reuters report. Both sides have repeatedly accused the other of endangering the facility, so the specific responsibility remains disputed. Diesel is safety-critical because emergency generators cool nuclear fuel whenever the plant’s external power connections fail. The IAEA maintains observers at the site, which is not currently generating electricity.

The Kremlin welcomed President Donald Trump’s request that Ukraine stop striking Russian diesel facilities. Oil holds above $107 as the Saudi pipeline remains offline

Brent crude was up more than 2% at about $107.33 a barrel Monday, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate traded near $102.56. The increase followed attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and ships in the Middle East, with the East–West pipeline still unavailable as a major bypass route. Stored crude at Yanbu may soften the immediate impact, but a prolonged outage would put about 4% of global supply at risk. The sustained rise is feeding inflation concerns just as central banks weigh further rate increases.

Iran names 77 vessels for possible Hormuz penalties

Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority issued an updated list of 77 vessels it says violated passage protocols in the Strait of Hormuz. The authority said listed ships could face fines, detention or confiscation and warned insurers and classification societies against servicing them. Preliminary tracking data showed commodity-vessel transits through the strait falling to single digits per day over the weekend, below a recent average of 14, though ships operating without AIS may not be counted. The move adds a legal and commercial layer of uncertainty to an already disrupted global energy route.

Oil markets move from crisis sprint to depleted-buffer marathon

A new Reuters Breakingviews analysis says the prolonged Iran conflict has shifted the oil market from managing a short disruption to coping with structural strain. Global inventories have fallen sharply, refined-fuel shipments are constrained and alternative Saudi export routes have become less dependable after attacks and Houthi gains near key waterways. Crude above $100 and acute diesel shortages leave less capacity to absorb another outage. The analysis concludes that logistics and depleted buffers, not just headline production totals, are now the central market risk.

→ Reuters Breakingviews — the oil market’s longer disruption

New Mexico’s Supreme Court fined defense attorney Stephen Aarons $5,000 after an appellate brief included fabricated police testimony and witnesses. A frontier-model slowdown could strengthen second-tier AI firms

A Reuters Breakingviews analysis argues that calls to slow frontier-model capability growth could redirect investment toward inference, deployment and companies applying existing models. That shift could help second-tier model providers and alternative chipmakers compete while reducing some of the advantage held by firms focused on the largest training runs. The underlying safety proposals remain unsettled and would require credible independent evaluation and international coordination. The economic implication is that slower frontier scaling would change where AI capital flows rather than necessarily shrink the market.

Why it matters to Nera: More spending on enterprise deployment raises the importance of privacy controls, auditable data boundaries and secure inference across a wider range of models and infrastructure providers.

Palantir and Nvidia restrict AI use over data-control concerns

Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen Hamilton are limiting use of advanced AI models for sensitive work because of data-security and intellectual-property concerns, according to a report cited by Reuters. Palantir is seeking irrevocable zero-data-retention commitments from Anthropic, while Nvidia restricts Anthropic models to less sensitive tasks and Booz Allen bars their use for proprietary cybersecurity work. The dispute shows that contractual data controls are becoming a competitive requirement rather than a secondary procurement detail.

Why it matters to Nera: Demand for enforceable zero-retention, boundary guarantees and audit evidence maps directly to privacy-preserving enterprise infrastructure.

EU draft would bar under-15s from social media and AI chatbots

The European Commission is preparing a proposal that would prohibit children under 15 from using social media, AI chatbots and online games without parental consent, according to a draft seen by Reuters. The draft also calls for stronger age-assurance systems and would limit engagement features aimed at minors. The measure remains a proposal and would still face legislative negotiations before becoming law. If adopted, it would expand the compliance burden for platforms that process children’s identity and behavioral data.

Why it matters to Nera: Age assurance increases the need for data minimization and privacy-preserving verification so platforms can prove eligibility without collecting unnecessary identity data.

West Oakland BART access changes begin today

Construction begins Monday on 240 affordable homes at West Oakland BART, reducing the station’s parking supply from about 440 spaces to 200 daily spaces. Bus stops, passenger pickup, bicycle parking and circulation routes are also moving, while accessible pickup remains in front of the station. Vehicle, pedestrian and bicycle access will stay open on the east and north sides, but southern access will be restricted. The larger transit-oriented plan calls for up to 762 homes, retail, offices and redesigned streets around the station.

Story 4 of 4

San Jose officer fired after misuse of Flock camera data

San Jose’s police chief said an officer was fired after using the department’s Flock license-plate-reader system from a personal device to locate a domestic-violence victim and share her location with the alleged abuser, who was the officer’s relative. The department referred the conduct for criminal review, but no charges were filed. San Jose has since barred personal-device access and added tighter auditing, while privacy advocates argue the incident demonstrates the risks of retaining searchable movement data at scale. The case is both a local public-trust failure and a concrete warning about access controls for sensitive surveillance systems.
