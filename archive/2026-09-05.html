Iranian tanker reportedly hit near Kharg Island

An Iranian oil tanker near Kharg Island was reportedly struck by four U.S. missiles, according to Iran’s semi-official Tasnim news agency. No casualties were reported and the crew was being evacuated, while U.S. Central Command had not immediately confirmed the attack. Because Kharg Island is central to Iran’s crude-export system, sustained military pressure there could affect oil prices, shipping risk and the fragile U.S.-Iran diplomatic track.

Families of foreign pilgrims missing in Tibet are pressing Chinese authorities for more information after the catastrophic border mudslides. CalPrivacy raises stakes for continuous compliance

CalPrivacy’s new enforcement advisory warns data brokers that inaccurate registry information can generate a $200 fine for every day it remains incorrect. The agency is treating disclosures as continuing operational obligations rather than annual paperwork and points to its expanding enforcement record as evidence those obligations will be tested. Privacy programs therefore need ongoing visibility into what data exists, how it is used and whether public representations remain accurate.

Why it matters to Nera: Continuous compliance increases the value of systems where sensitive-data use is technically constrained and auditable, strengthening the case for confidential computation as infrastructure rather than policy alone.

Story 2 of 2

DROP turns privacy rights into recurring operations

California data brokers must now access DROP at least every 45 days, download deletion lists, hash and match their own records, process requests and report outcomes back to the state. That turns a statutory privacy right into a recurring machine-readable workflow rather than a one-time legal exercise. More than half a million Californians have already enrolled, giving the system enough scale to become a meaningful operational compliance burden.

Why it matters to Nera: As privacy obligations become executable workflows, buyers will increasingly favor privacy technology that integrates directly with data operations.

Clippers punishment reshapes the L.A. landscape

The NBA’s punishment of the Clippers remains the biggest Los Angeles basketball story: a $30 million fine, five forfeited first-round picks and a one-year suspension for Steve Ballmer after the Kawhi Leonard cap investigation. The asset penalty stretches from 2029 through 2033, so the consequences could outlast the current roster by years. For the Lakers, the effect is indirect but meaningful because their crosstown rival now has far less flexibility to replenish an aging team.

Hiker rescued after mountain-lion report near San Jose

A hiker on the Boccardo Loop Trail in Sierra Vista Open Space Preserve was airlifted after reporting that a mountain lion was stalking him about two miles from the trailhead. The hiker was not injured, but limited cellphone coverage and concern about continuing on foot prompted a helicopter response. Mountain-lion attacks remain rare in California, but the episode is a useful reminder for local hikers to avoid traveling alone when possible and to know how to respond to wildlife encounters.
