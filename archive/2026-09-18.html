Bank of Japan raises rates to a 31-year high

The Bank of Japan raised its benchmark rate to 1.25%, its highest level in 31 years, as it continued moving away from decades of ultra-loose policy. The decision passed 7–2, with two board members dissenting, while inflation remains near the central bank’s 2% target. Markets had broadly expected the move, and the yen weakened after investors judged the action and guidance less forceful than some had hoped. Governor Kazuo Ueda’s explanation will shape expectations for how quickly Japan tightens further.

Russian drones struck a passenger bus near Nikopol and a train in the Mykolaiv region, killing five people and injuring seven, Ukrainian officials said. All 168 passengers and crew escaped from the train after it was hit. Buses and railways have become essential civilian infrastructure because Ukraine’s airspace remains closed. The attacks deepen concern that logistics and public transportation are becoming a central front in the war.

The disaster shows how the war’s danger persists even away from an active strike when displaced families have nowhere structurally safe to live.

Hormuz shipping remains far below its recent average

Only four commodity vessels passed through the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, compared with a 10-day average of 16, according to preliminary Kpler data. Some ships may be disabling transponders to reduce their visibility in the conflict zone, so the tracked count may understate actual traffic. The strait carried about one-fifth of global oil and gas before the Iran conflict. Persistently low transit volumes keep energy and shipping markets exposed even as Saudi pipeline repairs ease some immediate supply fears.

IEA says electrification remains the way forward despite AI jitters

International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol said electricity demand is growing three times faster than total energy demand and now attracts 61% of global energy investment. The IEA and South Korea launched RISE ASIA, which aims to help Asian economies reach 35% electrification by 2035. Birol also warned that a cold winter could intensify competition between Europe and Asia for costly LNG cargoes.

SK Hynix’s Solidigm weighs a U.S. NAND-chip factory

Story 3 of 5

EU proposes banning social media for children under 13

The European Commission proposed an EU Kids Act that would bar children under 13 from social media and require parental controls for accounts used by 13- to 15-year-olds. Platforms would need to disable addictive features, profiling-based feeds, infinite scroll, unsolicited stranger contact and AI companions aimed at minors. The proposal also requires robust age verification and could expose violators to fines of up to 6% of global revenue. Privacy-preserving age assurance will be a central implementation challenge if the measure advances.

Australia considers banning camera-equipped smart glasses in government buildings

Australia is considering restrictions on camera-equipped smart glasses in government buildings because of privacy and security risks from covert recording. Public Service Minister Katy Gallagher has asked for formal guidance, while the government plans to consult large employers on workplace AI rules. Similar limits have appeared in European schools and courts, but Australia’s proposal could become the first broad national-government workplace ban. The policy question is whether organizations can control ambient capture without eliminating accessibility benefits for people who rely on the devices.

French authorities intensify scrutiny of AI-enabled smart glasses

Paris prosecutors opened a criminal investigation after reports that people used AI-enabled smart glasses to secretly film women and post the videos online. France’s data-protection authority has also received workplace complaints as Meta’s discreet recording devices gain adoption. Regulators in Australia, Germany and other jurisdictions are considering or pursuing restrictions of their own. The cases highlight how weak capture indicators and rapid content distribution can turn wearable AI into a privacy and consent problem before victims can respond.
