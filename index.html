Canada retaliates in widening U.S. trade fight

Canada will impose dollar-for-dollar tariffs on selected U.S. goods beginning September 8 after negotiations collapsed and Washington imposed new 50% levies on Canadian products. The dispute raises costs for tightly integrated cross-border supply chains and complicates the future of the U.S.-Mexico-Canada trade agreement.

California’s DROP system turns privacy rights into workflow

California data brokers must now begin processing deletion requests through the state’s Delete Request and Opt-out Platform, with brokers required to access the system at least every 45 days. CalPrivacy is pairing the operational requirement with enforcement, including a $116,490 action against LocateSmarter and a $52,400 action against Cybba. Privacy rights are therefore becoming recurring data-processing obligations rather than primarily policy and disclosure requirements.

Why it matters to Nera: This creates a clearer market for infrastructure that can minimize data exposure, enforce controls and demonstrate compliant handling of sensitive information.

→ CalPrivacy — DROP processing requirements · CalPrivacy — LocateSmarter enforcement action · CalPrivacy — Cybba enforcement action

Duality pushes PETs into sovereign enterprise AI

Duality and IBM Sovereign Core are combining customer-operated sovereign infrastructure with privacy-preserving data collaboration. Zama expands FHE into financial products

Zama is pushing fully homomorphic encryption beyond infrastructure into confidential financial applications. Its confidential USDC vault with Morpho and Steakhouse Financial keeps individual deposit positions encrypted while preserving an auditable underlying strategy, and its Elliptic partnership adds compliance screening for confidential finance. Why it matters to Nera: Zama demonstrates an application-led commercialization strategy for FHE: pair confidentiality with a concrete financial product and the compliance tooling institutions already require.

California weighs nearly $100 million for Highway 37

California transportation officials are considering nearly $100 million for Highway 37 projects, including bridge repairs, improvements at Highway 121 and shoulder-running toll lanes. The work is an interim response to congestion and flooding on a corridor that planners warn could be permanently submerged by 2050 without major intervention. The larger long-term concept is an elevated causeway, but the full rebuild is projected to cost about $11 billion and take more than two decades.
