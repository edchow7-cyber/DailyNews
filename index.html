California’s CIPA overhaul may end thousands of tracking lawsuits

California changed part of its Invasion of Privacy Act in a way that could extinguish thousands of lawsuits over web-tracking tools. The law removes a private right of action from the pen-register provision often used against analytics and advertising technology. Privacy and labor groups argue that the change weakens a practical enforcement channel at a moment of expanding surveillance. Companies gain litigation relief, but regulators and plaintiffs will test what accountability remains under other statutes.
