One year on, Gaza ceasefire gains have stalled while fighting and reconstruction gaps persist

One year after President Trump introduced his Gaza plan, its first phase delivered a ceasefire and hostage-prisoner exchanges, but the larger settlement has largely stalled. Israeli forces still occupy nearly two-thirds of Gaza, strikes have continued, and Hamas has not disarmed.

Aid flows remain below the agreement’s target, while only $2.45 billion has been pledged against an estimated $71.4 billion reconstruction need. Israel’s October 27 election now adds another political deadline to a process that has preserved some humanitarian gains without resolving the conflict.

Yemeni government forces report retaking the Bab el-Mandeb coast

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces say a rapid advance pushed Houthi fighters from most of the coast around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and reached the outskirts of Mocha. The government says it now controls key positions at the mouth of a shipping lane that carries oil and other trade between the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

Reuters could not independently verify the full extent of the gains, and the Houthis dispute the government’s account while reporting advances near Taiz. The renewed fighting has displaced roughly 184,000 people and keeps a strategic chokepoint—and an already severe humanitarian crisis—at the center of the regional conflict.

U.S. trade deficit widens 13.7% as imports surge

The U.S. trade deficit increased to $105.6 billion in August from a revised $92.8 billion in July, exceeding economists’ expectations. Imports rose 4.3% as domestic buyers brought in more goods, while exports increased a smaller 1.4%.

The gap was the largest since March 2025 and puts trade on course to subtract from third-quarter growth. The composition matters as much as the headline: stronger imports can signal firm demand, but the imbalance also exposes the economy to currency, tariff, and supply-chain shifts.

Governments worldwide weigh restrictions on smart glasses

Governments, courts, and employers are reviewing whether camera-equipped smart glasses should be restricted in public spaces and sensitive workplaces. Norway plans a temporary ban in places such as parks, museums, shopping centers, and public events, while Australia is considering limits in federal workplaces.

The concern is ambient capture: people may be photographed, filmed, or recorded without clear notice or practical consent. As Meta’s Ray-Bans and similar devices become mainstream, regulators are testing whether existing privacy rules are adequate for always-available cameras.

Smart-glasses scrutiny spreads beyond Norway

Smart-glasses restrictions are becoming a cross-jurisdiction privacy issue rather than a single-country experiment. Norway’s proposed public-place ban joins reviews in Australia and elsewhere over devices that can quietly capture faces, voices, confidential material, and location context.

For privacy-enhancing technology teams, the design challenge is concrete: visible capture signals, local processing, minimization, bystander controls, and enforceable retention limits need to work before data leaves the device. The regulatory direction suggests that consent and notice cannot remain buried in the wearer’s app settings.

Golden Gate Bridge begins nightly lane closures for a decade-long seismic retrofit

Three of the Golden Gate Bridge’s six lanes will close overnight this week as crews begin assembling suspended work platforms for a major seismic retrofit. Weeknight work runs from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m., with Saturday closures extending to 7 a.m.; one southbound and two northbound lanes will remain open.

The first phase reinforces the north and south side spans, while main-span work is scheduled to begin in 2031. Drivers should expect recurring overnight constraints as the bridge enters a project expected to last roughly a decade.
