Iran and U.S. trade their biggest shipping attacks of the war

Iran and the United States carried out their most intense exchange of maritime attacks since the conflict began, striking tankers and other vessels near the Strait of Hormuz. Iran said its attacks were retaliation for U.S. strikes on Iranian tankers, while the Revolutionary Guard also launched missiles toward a U.S. base in Jordan. At least one seafarer was killed and energy flows through the region tightened further. The escalation matters well beyond the battlefield because Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint for global oil supply.

Russian strikes kill civilians at Ukraine border and Sumy

Russian drones hit the Starokozache crossing on the Ukraine–Moldova border, killing two civilians and injuring three while disrupting an increasingly important land-trade route. A separate strike on a shopping center in Sumy killed three more people. Kyiv also reported renewed attacks after a brief diplomatic lull. Together, the strikes show Russia continuing to pressure both civilian infrastructure and the transport links Ukraine relies on as Black Sea routes remain vulnerable.

Brent breaks above $100 as Hormuz risk intensifies

Brent crude climbed above $100 a barrel for the first time since July as attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure raised the probability of sustained supply disruption. West Texas Intermediate also rose sharply, while physical crude and refined-fuel markets showed signs of tightening. The move is important because the oil shock is now large enough to feed directly into inflation expectations, consumer fuel costs and central-bank decisions. A prolonged disruption around Hormuz would amplify all three effects.

→ Reuters — Brent rises above $100

Stocks fall as oil and Treasury yields climb

U.S. and global equities weakened while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to its highest level in nearly three years. Investors are repricing the combination of higher energy costs, persistent inflation risk and less certainty that central banks can ease policy quickly. Gold rose while the dollar was mixed, a pattern consistent with elevated geopolitical and rate uncertainty. The key near-term question is whether oil above $100 proves temporary or becomes embedded in the inflation outlook.

U.S. accuses Chinese AI firms of industrial-scale copying

U.S. officials accused several Chinese AI developers of using distillation and related methods to reproduce capabilities from leading American models at scale. The dispute is partly geopolitical, but the underlying technical issue is broader: valuable model behavior can be extracted through interfaces even when weights and training data remain protected. That makes output controls, rate-pattern monitoring and query governance increasingly important. The episode will intensify debate over the boundary between legitimate model learning and misappropriation.

Story 2 of 2

Meta’s Muse expands the trust boundary for AI agents

Meta’s Muse is designed to act across connected services such as email, calendars, shopping and payments rather than simply answer questions. That increases the value of agentic AI but also broadens the number of sensitive systems a single workflow can touch. Meta says app permissions and a separate safety system constrain the agent, yet reported testing problems underline how difficult authorization and reliability become once AI can take actions. The industry is moving from “what can the model know?” toward “what can the model do, and with whose data?”

Story 1 of 2

Model extraction becomes an enterprise privacy/IP issue

The U.S.–China distillation dispute highlights that proprietary AI value can leak through model outputs even when the underlying model is never directly exposed. Enterprises deploying internal copilots and specialized models face the same risk at smaller scale: repeated queries can reveal sensitive logic, derived information or valuable behavior. Traditional encryption at rest does not address that problem because the leakage occurs during use. This pushes confidential computation and policy-aware query controls closer to the core AI architecture.

Why it matters to Nera: Nera can broaden its positioning from “protecting input data” to “protecting sensitive computation and derived results.” That is a stronger enterprise story as model IP itself becomes a governed asset.

→ Reuters — model distillation dispute

Story 2 of 2

Cross-app agents sharpen the case for confidential computation

An AI agent that can reach email, payments and business applications must operate across multiple trust boundaries at once. The more systems it can access, the more damaging an overbroad permission, compromised prompt or mistaken action can become. Enterprises will need ways to let agents compute on sensitive information without automatically exposing every underlying record to the model or service provider. That is precisely the kind of architectural pressure that can make PETs operational rather than merely compliance-oriented.

Why it matters to Nera: Agentic AI creates a concrete buyer story for confidential workflows: return the minimum useful result, enforce policy at computation time, and reduce raw-data exposure across connected systems.

→ Reuters — Meta Muse

Heat and unhealthy ozone peak across the Bay Area

A Heat Advisory and Spare the Air alert are both in effect as temperatures run roughly 10 to 20 degrees above normal. Inland locations are reaching triple digits while San Francisco and the shoreline are also unusually warm. Stagnant air is allowing ozone concentrations to build, especially in the Santa Clara Valley and eastern parts of the region. The Air District recommends moving outdoor exercise to early morning before ozone levels rise.

BART restored after Daly City fire, with residual slowdowns

BART says full system service was restored late Tuesday after the Daly City substation and trackside fire caused major disruptions during the evening commute. Trains were still running at reduced speeds between Colma and Daly City, producing delays of roughly 10 minutes through that segment. The update is materially better than last night’s shutdown, even though the corridor is not fully back to normal operating speed.

Angel Island burn paused over air quality

Officials canceled Wednesday’s planned continuation of the Angel Island prescribed burn after smoke from Tuesday’s work lingered around the Bay. Nearly 62 acres had been treated before the pause.
