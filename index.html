Top Headlines

Democratic midterm sweep redraws Washington’s balance of power

Democrats secured control of Congress after voters punished Republicans over inflation, tariffs, and energy costs tied to the Iran war. The results have set off Republican infighting and are already changing expectations for investigations, nominees, and legislation in the next Congress. President Trump spent Thursday emphasizing Washington construction and décor while defending key allies, a contrast that underscored the administration’s unsettled response to the electoral rebuke.

Source: Associated Press

Paraguay’s president resigns as a digital-bank collapse becomes a national crisis

President Santiago Peña resigned after the collapse of digital lender ueno froze access to accounts for roughly three million customers and shattered his support inside the ruling Colorado Party. Lawmakers had accused Peña of conflicts of interest, misuse of pension funds, and helping shield the bank from scrutiny; he denies wrongdoing. The crisis has also forced out the central-bank chief, weakened Paraguay’s currency, and raised urgent questions about the fate of roughly $850 million in public funds.

Source: Reuters via GMA News

Qatar presses for a negotiated off-ramp in the Middle East

President Trump and Qatar’s Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani discussed efforts to de-escalate regional tensions and protect maritime navigation and energy supplies. Qatar remains a key intermediary between Washington and Tehran, while Egypt and Gulf governments are separately urging flexibility and compromise. The diplomatic activity matters because renewed military action could quickly threaten shipping, oil prices, and regional security.

Source: Associated Press

Business

Markets try to stabilize after a historic AI-led rout

Nasdaq futures rose Friday after Thursday’s 5.5% plunge erased about $2.5 trillion from the index and pushed it into correction territory. Investors are questioning the durability and financing of the AI infrastructure boom while also pricing tighter regulation after the Democratic midterm victory. Next week’s inflation data and earnings from AI-exposed companies will test whether the rebound is durable or only a pause in a broader repricing.

Source: Reuters

The $6.3 billion Binance theft exposes the limits of crypto insurance

Insurers are expected to demand more specific security evidence from crypto companies after the largest digital-asset theft on record. Investigators say a North Korea-linked group compromised engineers through a fake job interview and malicious advertising before draining Binance, while state-attack exclusions may complicate any claim. Industry experts do not expect insurers to abandon crypto, but they do expect tighter terms, clearer sublimits, and much closer scrutiny of correlated exposure.

Source: Reuters

Tech

OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber for authorized vulnerability research

OpenAI says GPT-6 Cyber improves exploit validation and patch development while following researcher-defined scope more reliably than its predecessor. The company reports gains on ExploitGym, 948 validated Linux privilege-escalation exploits, and 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, with many fixes already released. The release raises the ceiling for automated defense but also puts governance, customer vetting, and strict authorization boundaries at the center of frontier-model deployment.

Source: OpenAI

U.S. agencies warn that hackers are targeting industrial refrigeration

Federal agencies say malicious actors are exploiting internet-accessible temperature and refrigeration controllers used by groceries and cold-storage operators. Successful attacks could spoil inventory, shorten equipment life, or create fire hazards, turning an ordinary connected controller into a physical-safety risk. Operators are being urged to remove controllers from the public internet, segment them from business networks, patch systems, and watch for unexpected changes.

Source: Reuters

Nera Watch

California moves universal opt-out signals toward formal rulemaking

The California Privacy Protection Agency’s November 5–6 meeting package includes draft text, notice documents, and staff work on opt-out preference signals such as Global Privacy Control. The proposed amendments would clarify when businesses must treat those signals as valid sale/share opt-outs and expand related metrics reporting. The board’s action will shape whether browser-level privacy choices become a more operationally precise and auditable compliance obligation.

Why this is important to Nera: Stronger opt-out duties increase demand for privacy-preserving analytics that can measure and honor consumer choices without rebuilding identity graphs. Nera can position confidential computation and FHE as infrastructure for compliant measurement, segmentation, and model evaluation under tighter data-use constraints.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency

NIST’s privacy-enhancing cryptography program maps deployment tradeoffs

NIST’s WPEC 2026 materials now collect 22 talks across fully homomorphic encryption, private-set intersection, zero-knowledge proofs, and privacy-preserving credentials. The program focuses not only on primitives but also on real use cases, auditability, leakage, delegation, and scheme selection. The result is a practical reference set for teams deciding which PET belongs in a production workflow rather than treating every privacy problem as the same cryptographic task.

Why this is important to Nera: NIST’s framing validates Nera’s market: customers need a decision layer that connects business requirements, threat models, and operational constraints to the right confidential-compute technique. The published deployment questions can sharpen Nera’s product packaging, benchmarks, and enterprise education around when FHE is the best fit and when a complementary PET should be used.

Source: NIST

Zama says confidential vaults now hold more than $100 million

Zama reports that shielded value on its protocol reached $122 million, including more than $102 million in confidential vaults as of October 31. Its October recap also highlights wallet integrations, confidential-token distribution, and institutional research that places FHE at 42% of the confidential-DeFi market. The figures are vendor-reported, but they are a meaningful commercialization signal for encrypted computation moving from developer tooling into live financial products.

Why this is important to Nera: Zama’s traction is both competitive pressure and market proof. Nera should study the low-friction vault and wallet integrations, differentiate on confidential AI and enterprise workloads beyond DeFi, and use the adoption data to accelerate partnerships with regulated customers that need privacy, auditability, and usable deployment economics.

Source: Zama

Bay Area

An $11.4 million Nvidia shipment disappears after leaving Oakland

Oakland police are investigating the theft of six pallets of Nvidia hardware that were bound for Fort Worth, Texas. The shipment left Oakland on August 29 in a truck identified with a food-transport company, but investigators later lost track of the vehicle in Southern California. The case illustrates how the value and scarcity of AI hardware are attracting sophisticated cargo-theft operations alongside conventional cyber threats.

Source: ABC7 Bay Area

Bay Area voters approve long-term funding for BART, Muni, and Caltrain

Voters in five counties approved a regional sales tax expected to support more than 20 transit agencies for 14 years, while San Francisco separately approved a 15-year Muni parcel tax. The measures avert the immediate threat of deep service cuts driven by large structural deficits at BART, Muni, and Caltrain. Passage buys time, but agencies still face the harder work of improving reliability, rebuilding ridership, and proving that new revenue produces a more useful regional network.

Source: The San Francisco Standard
