Top Headlines

Congo’s Ebola death toll passes 5,000

Government data show more than 5,000 deaths in the Democratic Republic of Congo, making the outbreak the country’s deadliest and the world’s second-largest on record. Eight of 26 provinces have been affected, and many deaths occurred outside treatment centers. Confirmed cases in Kenya and Nigeria have intensified fears of a wider regional emergency. Containment now depends on surveillance, trust, vaccination, and treatment capacity moving faster than transmission.

Source: Associated Press, October 31

France deploys 70,000 police for a third weekend of protests

Authorities expect large demonstrations over living costs, education, public services, and President Emmanuel Macron’s government. The youth-led movement has spread beyond its student origins, while labor groups are calling for a nationwide strike on November 5. Extraordinary controls have drawn civil-liberties objections. The weekend will test whether the movement broadens or fractures under heavy security.

Source: Reuters, October 31

Business

October ends with a sharp repricing of the AI trade

The Nasdaq posted its biggest decline since June as investors reassessed the scale and financing of hyperscaler AI spending. Intel and Reddit each fell 29%, while Sandisk dropped 13%. The question is shifting from whether AI demand exists to which companies can monetize infrastructure quickly enough to justify the capital. Oil above $100 and weaker risk appetite added pressure across asset classes.

Source: Reuters, October 31

DoorDash’s $17 billion Instacart acquisition raises the delivery stakes

DoorDash agreed to pay $65 a share for Instacart, with at least three-quarters of the consideration in cash. The companies plan to retain Instacart’s brand while combining restaurant, grocery, retail, and merchant technology. The strategic case is scale against larger commerce platforms, but the long closing window signals significant review. Integration risk centers on merchant economics, consumer choice, and whether the network produces durable savings.

Source: Instacart and DoorDash, October 29

Tech

AI is becoming central to U.S. warfare—and its errors are consequential

The Pentagon is combining intelligence feeds with commercial models to accelerate analysis and targeting. Officials credit the systems with synthesizing more information, but reported failures include a mistaken assessment that nearly triggered a wrongful interdiction and decisions based on outdated maps. Faster cycles cannot eliminate strategic error, civilian harm, or vendor dependence. Deployment is outpacing consensus on review, auditability, and accountability.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Foxconn prepares another OpenAI collaboration

Foxconn Chairman Young Liu says the manufacturer is developing a new partnership with OpenAI after meeting Sam Altman. The relationship would extend earlier work on data-center racks and components as AI servers become more than half of Foxconn’s cloud-and-networking revenue. Foxconn expects double-digit growth next year and is adding humanoid robots to Texas facilities. AI infrastructure spending is spreading from chips and clouds into manufacturing systems.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Nera Watch

EU Council advances changes to privacy, cookies, and cyber reporting

The Council’s Digital Omnibus position would clarify pseudonymization, modernize cookie rules, and create national entry points for cyber incidents. It also adds safeguards around sensitive data used for biometric verification and AI systems. Negotiations with Parliament follow after lawmakers adopt their position. The final text could change compliance workflows without reducing the need for demonstrable safeguards.

Why this is important to Nera: Buyers will want privacy controls that survive regulatory simplification. Nera can map confidential AI and FHE to pseudonymization, sensitive-data processing, breach containment, and trade-secret protection while separating technical protection from legal duties.

Source: Council of the EU, October 21

NIST continues building the foundation for useful FHE standards

NIST classifies fully homomorphic encryption alongside MPC, zero-knowledge proofs, and private set intersection in its privacy-enhancing cryptography program. Its work focuses on practical development and future standards, not a purely academic primitive. Enterprise adoption increasingly depends on interoperable security definitions, benchmarks, and procurement language. Standards participation is becoming part of go-to-market credibility.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera should align workload benchmarks, parameter choices, and security claims with NIST vocabulary. That strengthens diligence, makes competitor comparisons concrete, and positions compute-without-decrypting as governed infrastructure rather than a black-box feature.

Source: NIST Privacy-Enhancing Cryptography

Locktera turns confidential-AI claims into a named customer case

Locktera says BCaster is using its platform to protect AI image and video data with encryption, policy controls, and auditing. The deployment moves the competitor’s message from technical possibility to operational proof. It broadens the competitive frame from encrypted retrieval toward persistent governance. Enterprise buyers will increasingly demand both cryptography and integration evidence.

Why this is important to Nera: Locktera is building customer proof in Nera’s category. Nera should compare trust models, FHE depth, performance, auditability, and integration friction, then accelerate a reference deployment showing why “Nera never sees secrets” is operationally distinct.

Source: Locktera, October 21

Lakers / NBA

Warriors erase a 25-point deficit and rout the Lakers 112–95

Stephen Curry scored 31 points and hit eight three-pointers as Golden State flipped the NBA Cup opener with a 33–8 run across the third and fourth quarters. Luka Dončić led all scorers with 37, but Los Angeles could not turn its explosive first quarter into control. The Warriors outscored the Lakers 66–33 from beyond the arc. Los Angeles hosts Toronto on Sunday.

Source: NBA.com, October 31

The bench and turnover margins expose the Lakers’ immediate problem

Golden State’s reserves produced 50 points while the Lakers’ bench managed only seven. Los Angeles also committed 20 turnovers, surrendering the rhythm built during its 38-point opening quarter. The result points to lineup instability when Dončić rests or draws concentrated pressure. Sunday provides a quick test of secondary creation and ball security.

Source: Reuters, October 31

Bay Area

High tides keep Bay shoreline flooding risk elevated

A Coastal Flood Advisory remains in effect through Saturday for the Pacific coast and Sunday for bay shorelines. The San Francisco gauge is forecast around 1.5 feet above normal at Saturday afternoon’s high tide, with up to 1.9 feet of inundation possible in low-lying locations. Road closures, coastal erosion, and flooding around Marin, Palo Alto, Martinez, and the Embarcadero remain possible. Drivers should avoid barricaded water.

Source: ABC7 Bay Area live updates

The stakes make it the region’s defining sports event of the day.

Source: ABC7 Bay Area, October 30
