Gulf energy disruption hits Bangladesh and Pakistan

The worsening Gulf crisis is disrupting energy supplies in Bangladesh and Pakistan as traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea routes remains constrained. Asian LNG prices have climbed toward $30 per million BTUs, forcing import-dependent buyers into a costly spot market. Bangladesh is facing blackouts and factory slowdowns, including in its garment industry, while Pakistan is using fuel subsidies to soften the shock. The disruption shows how quickly a regional conflict can reach households and export industries far beyond the Gulf.

IEA says electrification remains the way forward despite AI jitters

International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol said electricity demand is growing three times faster than total energy demand and now attracts 61% of global energy investment. The IEA and South Korea launched RISE ASIA, which aims to help Asian economies reach 35% electrification by 2035. Birol also warned that a cold winter could intensify competition between Europe and Asia for costly LNG cargoes.
