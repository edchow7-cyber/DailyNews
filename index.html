French student protests deepen as schools stay blockaded

French teenagers blockaded schools and marched through cities on Thursday as anger over teacher shortages and deteriorating buildings persisted. Police used tear gas at a barricaded Paris high school, while related unrest spread into Belgium. The movement is becoming a broader test of whether officials can turn promises about public services into visible improvements.

White House leans on children’s investment accounts in its economic pitch

President Donald Trump is promoting government-created investment accounts for Americans under 18 as Republicans confront continued anxiety about household costs. The plan is designed as a long-term ownership program rather than an immediate answer to fuel, healthcare or housing expenses. IMF warns that energy, debt and AI risks are converging

The IMF’s managing director warned that an energy shock, rising public debt and uneven AI gains could weaken global growth at the same time. Governments have less fiscal room than they did during earlier crises, making new shocks harder to absorb. AI investment may lift productivity, but the gains and transition costs are likely to be distributed unevenly. The warning is less about one imminent downturn than about several risks reinforcing each other.

California’s CIPA overhaul may end thousands of tracking lawsuits

California changed part of its Invasion of Privacy Act in a way that could extinguish thousands of lawsuits over web-tracking tools. The law removes a private right of action from the pen-register provision often used against analytics and advertising technology. Privacy and labor groups argue that the change weakens a practical enforcement channel at a moment of expanding surveillance. Companies gain litigation relief, but regulators and plaintiffs will test what accountability remains under other statutes.
