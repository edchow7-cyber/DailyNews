Nvidia says the AI spending boom still has years to run

Nvidia forecast roughly 70% revenue growth next fiscal year after reporting another enormous quarter, reinforcing the argument that AI infrastructure spending remains in expansion rather than near a peak. Data-center revenue reached about $89 billion, and demand is broadening beyond the largest cloud providers to AI labs and newer infrastructure companies. Shares rose sharply as investors interpreted the outlook as confirmation that the transition from experimentation to production is accelerating. The result validates capital-spending plans across chips, data centers, power and networking.

Story 1 of 3

California DROP crosses 500,000 users and tens of millions of deletions

California says more than 500,000 residents have now registered for its Delete Request and Opt-out Platform. Data brokers began mandatory processing on August 1 and have already reported deleting tens of millions of records. The milestone moves DROP from a policy experiment into a high-volume operational system with real compliance consequences. It demonstrates that privacy regulation increasingly creates recurring data-processing workloads, not just disclosure obligations.

Why it matters to Nera: The market is moving toward systems that can prove sensitive data was matched, processed, minimized and governed correctly at scale. That strengthens the value proposition for privacy-preserving infrastructure that reduces exposure while still enabling useful computation.

Duality attaches PETs directly to IBM sovereign-AI infrastructure

Duality Technologies is integrating privacy-preserving data collaboration with IBM Sovereign Core, allowing customers to analyze sensitive distributed data without exposing raw information. IBM provides the customer-operated control plane for identity, keys, logs and operations, while Duality supplies the privacy-enhancing computation layer. The packaging places PETs inside an enterprise buying category — sovereign AI — rather than presenting them as standalone cryptography. Why it matters to Nera: Duality is demonstrating a useful go-to-market pattern: attach privacy technology to a business requirement that already has urgency and budget. Nera should continue framing confidential computation around concrete finance and analytics workflows rather than around FHE itself.

Story 3 of 3

Zama pairs FHE confidentiality with compliance screening

Zama is partnering with blockchain-intelligence company Elliptic to bring compliance screening into confidential financial applications. Zama’s FHE keeps balances and transaction values encrypted, while Elliptic provides the risk and compliance layer institutions need before using confidential finance in regulated markets. The combination directly addresses a common objection to privacy technology: confidentiality cannot come at the expense of AML or sanctions controls. FHE commercialization is moving toward complete regulated workflows rather than cryptographic capability alone.

Why it matters to Nera: Zama is bundling privacy with the surrounding controls required for adoption. For Nera, the analogous opportunity is to make confidential analytics fit naturally into existing enterprise governance, audit and compliance processes.

Tropical moisture brings a rare August rain chance to the Bay Area

Moisture from former Tropical Storm Iselle is moving into Northern California, giving the Bay Area a small but unusual chance of August showers. The humidity could help fire-containment efforts, but clouds may interfere with tonight’s partial lunar eclipse viewing. Inland temperatures are still expected to reach the 90s.
