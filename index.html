IMF chief warns energy shock, debt and AI risks threaten global growth

The IMF’s Kristalina Georgieva says persistently high energy prices, record public debt and the AI investment boom are creating a difficult mix for the world economy. Oil near $100 and possible LNG disruptions are feeding inflation while governments have less fiscal room to respond. She still sees AI adding as much as half a percentage point to annual growth, but warned about market concentration, labor displacement and cyber risk. The speech frames next week’s IMF and World Bank meetings around resilience rather than a clean soft landing.

IEA members consider an emergency release from strategic reserves

The International Energy Agency is meeting Wednesday to discuss a coordinated release of emergency oil stocks as conflict-driven supply risks keep prices elevated. A release could provide near-term relief, but it cannot replace damaged infrastructure or permanently offset constrained production. The size, timing and level of member participation will determine whether markets treat the move as meaningful.

Houthi missiles and drones hit Aden airport as Yemen fighting escalates

Yemen’s Houthis attacked Aden International Airport with ballistic missiles and explosive drones, disrupting an incoming flight and forcing emergency measures. The strike came as Saudi-backed government forces pursued a counteroffensive near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, now even more important to energy traffic because of disruptions around Hormuz. Recent fighting has displaced more than 200,000 people and reopened one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises. The immediate question is whether coalition gains hold or the conflict widens further into Saudi infrastructure and regional shipping.

India raises rates for the first time in nearly four years

The Reserve Bank of India raised its policy rate by 25 basis points and shifted to a calibrated-tightening stance. It also lifted both its inflation and growth forecasts, signaling that strong domestic demand is colliding with renewed price pressure. The move puts India into the broader global tightening conversation just as markets had been leaning toward easier policy elsewhere. Future increases will depend on how long energy and food shocks persist without breaking growth.

OpenAI says its continuing investigation into agent-related intrusions is costing about $500,000 a day and requires analysis of roughly 50 petabytes of data.
