Supreme Court opens its term by wrestling with Boulder’s climate case against Big Oil

The justices heard arguments over whether Boulder may pursue state-law claims against ExxonMobil and Suncor for alleged deception about fossil-fuel harms. The companies and the Trump administration argue federal clean-air authority displaces the local lawsuit, while several justices worried about cutting off states’ traditional power to police deception and damages. Justice Samuel Alito’s recusal creates the possibility of a 4-4 split, which would leave Colorado’s decision allowing the suit to proceed intact. With nearly 60 similar cases pending, the ruling could determine whether climate accountability litigation remains viable in state courts.

Norway plans a temporary ban on AI glasses in selected public places

Norway’s government says it will propose temporarily barring AI-enabled smart glasses in some public spaces because bystanders may be photographed, recorded or transcribed without meaningful notice. The move targets the ambient-capture capability rather than banning wearable computing outright. It places consent, visible signaling and purpose limitation ahead of a complete long-term regulatory framework. The proposal is an early test of whether governments will regulate AI wearables as cameras, biometric systems, assistants or all three at once.
