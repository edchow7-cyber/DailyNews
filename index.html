Iran attacks a U.S. base in Jordan and ships near Hormuz

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard launched ballistic missiles at a U.S. military base near Al Azraq in Jordan and attacked ten ships in and around the Strait of Hormuz, including two U.S. vessels. Jordanian and U.S. officials said most missiles were intercepted and reported no casualties at the base, but the maritime attacks deepen the threat to one of the world’s most important energy corridors. The escalation follows U.S. strikes on Iranian tankers and arrives just after Houthi attacks on Saudi cities and oil infrastructure, widening the regional conflict again.

Qualcomm granted Amazon warrants for an equity stake worth about $4 billion as part of a collaboration on custom AI-inference chips. Story 1 of 3

Australia proposes a user choice over social-media algorithms

Australia’s proposed “My Feed, My Way” law would require platforms to let users choose between algorithmically personalized feeds and posts only from accounts they follow. Platforms would also have to document how they address harms, with penalties reaching A$109.2 million for noncompliance. The proposal follows the European approach of treating profiling as a choice that must be visible and usable, rather than an unavoidable default.

Why it matters to Nera: The regulatory direction favors architectures that can personalize or measure outcomes while minimizing exposure of raw behavioral data. Nera can frame confidential analytics as a way to preserve useful computation when consent narrows the data platforms may freely centralize.

Story 2 of 3

Meta’s cross-app agent raises a new privacy/control problem

Meta’s Muse can reach into email, calendars, payments and other connected services to complete tasks rather than merely answer questions. That creates a substantially larger trust boundary: a useful agent may need access to multiple sensitive systems at once, and a bad action can propagate across them. Meta says users retain app-level controls and that a safety agent supervises Muse, but reported internal security issues show why authorization and data minimization are becoming central design requirements for enterprise agents.

Why it matters to Nera: For Nera, agentic workflows strengthen the case for computing over sensitive information without exposing all underlying data to the agent or its provider. Confidential analytics and policy-enforced access can become part of an enterprise architecture that lets agents act on useful results while reducing raw-data exposure.

Story 3 of 3

U.S.–China AI dispute puts model extraction in the spotlight

The U.S. government’s allegations against Chinese AI companies focus on distillation: using outputs from stronger models to train cheaper systems that reproduce some of their behavior. Regardless of the geopolitical dispute, the episode highlights a practical enterprise problem—valuable model behavior can leak through interfaces even when model weights and training data remain private. Protecting AI intellectual property therefore requires controls around queries, outputs, rate patterns and sensitive derived information, not only encryption of stored files.

Why it matters to Nera: For Nera, this broadens the privacy story from protecting customer data to protecting the computation itself. PET-based controls can help enterprises collaborate with models and counterparties while revealing less about proprietary inputs, outputs and intermediate results.

→ Reuters — model distillation becomes a national-security issue

Officials canceled the second day of a planned prescribed burn on Angel Island after Tuesday’s fire left smoke lingering around the Bay.
