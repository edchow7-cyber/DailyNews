Top Headlines

Israel votes in its first election since the October 7 attacks

Israelis went to the polls at the fastest early-turnout pace in nearly half a century, choosing a government in the first national election since the October 7, 2023 attacks. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's political survival is tied to voters' judgment of the wars that followed in Gaza, Lebanon, and Iran. Long lines signal unusually high engagement, but a fragmented result could still lead to prolonged coalition bargaining. The outcome will shape security policy, regional diplomacy, and the domestic reckoning over the failures preceding the attacks.

Nordic leaders reopen direct contact with Putin

Finland's president and Norway's prime minister called Vladimir Putin to press for an end to Russia's war in Ukraine. The conversation broke with the practice of most Western European leaders, who have largely avoided direct contact with the Kremlin. Both countries border Russia, strongly back Ukraine, and said they were conveying messages connected to expected negotiations rather than speaking for Europe. The call creates a diplomatic channel, but the leaders said significant differences remain and any settlement must include Kyiv.

A localized Oleshky ceasefire begins while Kyiv remains under attack

Russia and Ukraine began a narrow ceasefire around occupied Oleshky to permit humanitarian deliveries and civilian departures. The pause does not extend across the wider front, and Russian strikes continued elsewhere, including Kyiv. That contrast makes the operation both a practical lifeline and a test of whether limited agreements can survive amid ongoing war. Success could support future humanitarian arrangements without implying progress toward a general truce.

Business

Markets rally before central-bank decisions

Asian shares rose after an AI-led Wall Street rally, with South Korea's Kospi jumping more than 3% and Japan's Nikkei gaining more than 2%. Investors are waiting for decisions from the Federal Reserve and other major central banks, alongside a dense calendar of corporate earnings. The Fed is widely expected to leave rates unchanged, making its guidance on inflation and future tightening more important than the decision itself. High borrowing costs remain a central vulnerability for capital-intensive AI and data-center projects.

Anthropic files for a long-awaited IPO

Anthropic has filed for an initial public offering, moving one of the largest private AI companies toward the public markets. The filing brings the scale of the company's revenue growth, losses, infrastructure needs, and governance structure into public view. Investors will have to decide whether rapid adoption can justify enormous training and compute costs. The deal is also a test of the market's appetite for a pure-play frontier-model company after a volatile month for AI-linked stocks.

Oil and rates remain the economic pressure points

Oil prices eased but remained elevated as traders watched diplomatic efforts around the Iran conflict. At the same time, the Federal Reserve's policy meeting keeps borrowing costs at the center of the market outlook. Expensive energy can feed inflation just as central banks try to determine whether growth is slowing enough to hold or reverse recent tightening. That combination is especially difficult for heavily financed infrastructure programs whose returns depend on years of stable demand.

Tech

Big Tech earnings put the AI boom on trial

Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon enter earnings week with nearly $1.7 trillion in combined backlog and as much as $600 billion in expected annual spending. Investors are no longer rewarding investment announcements by themselves; they want proof that cloud and AI demand can produce durable margins and cash flow. Competition from chatbots and personal agents is also putting new pressure on Google's core search business. The results will influence the financing climate for suppliers, data-center developers, and AI startups far beyond the three companies.

Cloud forecasts are strong, but cash flow is the test

Analysts expect Google Cloud revenue to rise about 95%, Azure about 48%, and AWS about 38% in the September quarter. Those forecasts would show powerful demand, but Alphabet and Amazon are still expected to burn cash while Microsoft may generate more than $8 billion. Microsoft will disclose Azure revenue in dollars for the first time under a revamped reporting structure. The contrast between growth and cash generation will help investors distinguish operating leverage from infrastructure-fueled expansion.

Telecom operators tighten AI-agent guardrails

AT&T and T-Mobile are increasing cybersecurity controls as AI agents gain access to customer data and network-management systems. Executives describe encryption, multifactor authentication, monitoring, human oversight, and verification of agent requests as necessary safeguards. Both carriers already use AI in operations, but fully autonomous networks remain years away. Their caution shows that deployment speed will depend on identity, authorization, observability, and recovery controls—not only model capability.

Nera Watch

NIST turns WPEC to private-set intersection

Day two of NIST's Workshop on Privacy-Enhancing Cryptography focuses on private-set intersection and privacy-preserving joins. The agenda moves from noisy and fuzzy identifiers to outsourced PSI, secret-shared SQL joins, distributed join-and-compute, and proximity testing. These are practical patterns for discovering overlap or joining records without exposing entire datasets. The program reflects an industry shift from isolated cryptographic primitives toward composable workflows that can support analytics and AI.

Why this is important to Nera: PSI and private joins complement Nera's confidential-AI and FHE positioning by solving the data-discovery step that often precedes protected inference. Nera can use the talks to identify integration points, benchmark partners, and customer workflows in finance, advertising, identity, and health where parties need to find shared records before computing on them.

Post-quantum PSI reaches practical latency

NIST's program includes a protocol for small-set private-set intersection built from standardized post-quantum primitives such as ML-KEM. The presenters report latency as low as 0.25 milliseconds per item and communication as low as 1.67 KiB per item. The result suggests that future-proof privacy can be practical in bounded matching workloads, though symmetric-key operations become a new bottleneck. It also gives buyers a concrete way to ask whether privacy products are planning for cryptographic migration rather than only today's threat model.

Why this is important to Nera: Enterprise and government customers will increasingly expect a post-quantum roadmap across the entire confidential-computing stack. Nera should evaluate whether its protocols, key management, dependencies, and performance claims remain credible under post-quantum components—and use that assessment to differentiate against competitors that treat migration as an afterthought.

India's privacy law faces a press-freedom challenge

A coalition of Indian publishers has asked the Supreme Court to overturn provisions of a privacy law scheduled to take effect in May 2027. The publishers argue that consent, deletion, and government-access rules could impede investigative reporting and expose confidential sources. The dispute illustrates how privacy governance can collide with legitimate uses when the system relies on broad legal permissions or exemptions. Courts and regulators will be looking for mechanisms that preserve accountability while minimizing unnecessary disclosure.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera can position confidential AI, auditable access controls, and encrypted processing as technical ways to separate permitted computation from raw-data exposure. The opportunity is strongest where customers must reconcile privacy, journalism, compliance, or public-interest obligations; the threat is that overly broad statutory exemptions may reduce short-term demand for privacy-preserving infrastructure.

Bay Area

A Muni stabbing leaves a suspect at large

A person was stabbed on a Muni bus near Laguna and McAllister streets early Monday, and the suspect fled before officers arrived. The victim was taken to a hospital with injuries that police said were not life-threatening. SFMTA says reported aggravated assaults on Muni are down sharply from 2019, but this was the second bus stabbing reported in roughly a month. Investigators are asking witnesses to contact San Francisco police.

San Francisco traces two TB cases to a Market Street club

San Francisco health officials have confirmed two active tuberculosis cases associated with the Crazy Horse club on Market Street. Contact tracing began after the first case was reported in early October, and officials say the investigation is continuing. The department has not publicly said whether the people involved were employees or patrons. Officials currently assess the risk to the general public as low but say they will update that judgment if conditions change.

A Russian Hill gas-line rupture prompts sheltering

A vehicle collision ruptured a gas line on Francisco Street near Polk, prompting a shelter-in-place order in Russian Hill. Two adults had minor injuries and did not require hospitalization. Fire officials issued the order as a precaution while crews assessed the leak and secured the area. The episode is a reminder that a routine traffic collision can quickly become a neighborhood-scale utility emergency in a dense part of the city.
