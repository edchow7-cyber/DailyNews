Isaias turns post-tropical after Florida landfall, but flooding and outages spread

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin as a Category 2 storm and weakened rapidly into a post-tropical cyclone while crossing southern Alabama. Maximum sustained winds were still about 65 mph early Saturday, and forecasters warned that storm surge, flash flooding, river flooding and tornadoes could persist across the Southeast. More than 430,000 customers in Florida and nearly 297,000 in Alabama had lost power, while downed trees and lines made travel dangerous. The storm’s classification changed, but the overnight safety message did not: stay sheltered and follow local emergency orders.

China orders tighter oversight of AI investment and safety risks

China’s government issued guidelines pairing aggressive support for advanced technology with tighter controls on speculative investment. The plan calls for more computing capacity, industrial AI pilots and breakthroughs in core technologies, but also for risk-warning and emergency-response systems that keep AI “safe, reliable and controllable.” Officials were warned against blind investment and “swarm-like” expansion into favored sectors. The policy is a useful counterpoint to Western debates: Beijing is promoting scale while explicitly treating bubbles and operational safety as governance problems.

California’s CIPA overhaul may end thousands of tracking lawsuits

California changed part of its Invasion of Privacy Act in a way that could extinguish thousands of lawsuits over web-tracking tools. The law removes a private right of action from the pen-register provision often used against analytics and advertising technology. Privacy and labor groups argue that the change weakens a practical enforcement channel at a moment of expanding surveillance. Companies gain litigation relief, but regulators and plaintiffs will test what accountability remains under other statutes.
