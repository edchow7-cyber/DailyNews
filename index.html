Alibaba plans to raise roughly $10.2 billion through a Hong Kong share placement and says all of the net proceeds will be invested in its full-stack AI capabilities. The AI investment boom increasingly depends not only on demand for computing but also on the cost of financing the data centers, power infrastructure and equipment needed to provide it. Story 1 of 3

California’s DROP system turns privacy rights into workflow

California’s Delete Request and Opt-out Platform now lets residents send a single deletion request to more than 600 registered data brokers. Starting August 1, brokers must access DROP at least every 45 days and begin processing those requests, turning a consumer privacy right into a recurring operational data workflow. CalPrivacy is reinforcing the mandate with enforcement: LocateSmarter was fined $116,490 for CCPA and Delete Act violations, while Cybba was ordered to pay $52,400 after failing to register by the required deadline.

Why it matters to Nera: Privacy regulation is moving from policies and consent screens into actual data-processing infrastructure. That creates a clearer market for technologies that can minimize exposure, enforce controls and prove that sensitive data was handled correctly.

Story 3 of 3

Zama expands FHE from infrastructure into financial products

Zama continues to push fully homomorphic encryption toward application-layer products, particularly confidential on-chain finance. Its commercialization strategy shows how FHE can move from an enabling technology into concrete products where confidentiality itself is part of the user value proposition. Financial applications are a particularly useful proving ground because they combine valuable data, strong privacy requirements and transactions that still need to be computed on while protected.

Why it matters to Nera: Zama demonstrates one path for turning FHE into a market category rather than selling cryptography directly. Nera is pursuing a different enterprise path, so watching where Zama finds repeatable demand can help distinguish which FHE use cases are becoming commercially mature.

Story 1 of 3

California commits nearly $100 million to Highway 37

California is putting nearly $100 million toward improvements on Highway 37, the heavily congested North Bay corridor connecting the East Bay with Sonoma County. The initial work includes Tolay Creek bridge repairs, widening near Highway 121 and converting shoulders along a roughly 10-mile stretch into toll lanes for transit and carpools. The spending is controversial because the low-lying highway also faces long-term flooding risk, raising questions about how much should be invested in the current roadway before a much more expensive climate-resilient replacement is built.
