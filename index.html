Typhoon Dujuan kills four as landslides and flooding hit eastern Japan

Torrential rain from Typhoon Dujuan triggered landslides and flooding along Japan’s Pacific coast, killing four people and leaving six missing, according to NHK. Kanagawa and Chiba were among the hardest-hit areas, with homes flooded or buried and rescue crews searching in Miura. About 45,000 households lost power as rivers overflowed and transport was disrupted. The casualty update materially advances Monday’s warning-stage story.

Sweden opens an election-fraud probe over alleged pre-marked ballots

Swedish prosecutors opened a preliminary investigation into allegations that pre-marked ballots benefited Left Party candidate Mohamed Abdukardir Ali in Borlänge. Ali said he would take leave from his parliamentary duties while the matter is examined, and prosecutors have not yet identified a suspect. Sweden’s Election Review Board could order a rerun in Dalarna, which would be the country’s first parliamentary rerun. The dispute is unlikely to alter the national balance of power, but it could reshuffle the region’s 11 seats.

EU renews Russia sanctions while removing two prominent billionaires

The European Union renewed sanctions on roughly 3,000 Russian people and entities for three years, replacing the previous six-month renewal cycle. As part of the compromise, the bloc removed billionaires Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman from the list after pressure from France and Luxembourg. Ukraine condemned the removals, while Latvia said it may impose national restrictions of its own. The agreement keeps the wider asset-freeze and travel-ban regime in force while exposing continued divisions inside the EU over enforcement.

Jet-powered Russian drones strain Ukraine’s air defenses

Russia’s newer jet-powered attack drones are flying faster and higher than propeller-driven Shaheds, making them harder for Ukraine’s mobile teams to intercept. Ukrainian officials told Reuters the interception rate for the jet variants is about 60%, compared with roughly 90% for older models. The drones are increasingly targeting ports, railways and steel plants as Kyiv faces a large defense-funding gap. Ukraine is racing to scale interceptor drones and other lower-cost countermeasures before the tactic expands further.

UN warns another 100,000 Yemenis may be displaced as fighting grows

More than 130,000 people have already fled their homes in Yemen this month as Houthi forces advance against the Saudi-backed government, the UN refugee agency said. Saudi Arabia restarts a pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz

Saudi Arabia resumed low-rate operations on its East-West oil pipeline after drone attacks shut it and halted loadings at Yanbu on September 13, sources told Reuters. The route can carry up to 4 million barrels per day—about 4% of global supply—from eastern fields to the Red Sea, bypassing the disrupted Strait of Hormuz. Operators are working toward full capacity while tankers reposition for renewed exports and ship-to-ship transfers. Brent crude fell more than $2 as the restart eased immediate supply concerns.

→ Reuters — Saudi Arabia restarts East-West oil pipeline

Gulf energy shock could drive a 70% jump in global biofuel output

A Chatham House and Forest Stewardship Council study projects global biofuel production could rise nearly 70% from 2025 levels by 2030 as governments respond to energy-security concerns and higher crude prices. The report warns that rapid expansion of first-generation fuels made from food crops could intensify food insecurity and land and water pressure. It estimates additional demand could require as much as 36 million hectares of land. Waste-based fuels offer a lower-impact path, but the study says policy safeguards and certification systems are not keeping pace.

EU proposes energy and water disclosure labels for data centers

The European Commission proposed rules requiring large data centers to disclose standardized measures of energy and water efficiency. Facilities above 500 kilowatts would also report how their water use relates to local stress and whether they can reuse waste heat or support the surrounding power grid. The proposal does not cap consumption, but the label could become a foundation for mandatory minimum standards. Europe expects data-center capacity to more than double by 2030 as it expands AI infrastructure.

Banks warn AI shopping agents could amplify fraud and privacy risks

A coalition including Bank of America, Capital One, NatWest, ING and Commonwealth Bank of Australia warned that AI shopping agents are advancing faster than consumer-protection standards. Their report highlights scams, mistaken purchases, exposure of payment credentials and agents steering customers toward weaker payment protections. The banks called for disclosure when AI participates in a transaction, stronger data safeguards, explainable choices and interoperability between services. For privacy-enhancing technology vendors, the report points directly to demand for controlled data sharing, auditable agent actions and minimized payment-data exposure.

Failed transit measures could more than double peak Bay Bridge delays

A SPUR-commissioned analysis says deep BART, Muni and Caltrain service cuts could push enough commuters into cars to more than double peak Bay Bridge travel times. The model also projects worsening congestion on Highway 101, Interstate 680 and through the Caldecott Tunnel if November transit-funding measures fail.
