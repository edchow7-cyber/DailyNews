Supreme Court opens a term that could expand Thomas and Alito’s influence

The U.S. Supreme Court begins its new term with a 6-3 conservative majority and major disputes over guns, religion, voting and immigration. Reuters reports that Justices Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito are positioned to exert particular influence as the court revisits issues they have pursued for years. The docket includes an assault-weapons challenge, a religious-schools dispute and an Arizona proof-of-citizenship voting case. The term could therefore reshape federal power and civil rights well beyond any single ruling.

China accelerates construction of a runway on disputed Antelope Reef

Satellite imagery shows Chinese work advancing on a runway at Antelope Reef in the Paracel Islands, with analysts estimating completion by mid-2027. A usable strip would expand Beijing’s ability to sustain aircraft and military operations across another part of the disputed South China Sea. Vietnam and Taiwan also claim the area, so the project adds a durable strategic fact rather than a temporary patrol. The construction underscores how incremental infrastructure can change the regional balance without a single dramatic confrontation.

U.S. job growth slows sharply, sending stocks higher and the dollar lower

Treasury yields reversed their initial decline, showing that the rate outlook remains unsettled even after the payroll miss. The market welcomed a possible pause, but the weak hiring number raises a less comfortable question about economic momentum entering the fourth quarter.

Trump keeps AI safeguards voluntary as public concern rises

The White House is promoting a voluntary safety accord with Nvidia, SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and Alphabet rather than binding federal rules. Polling cited by Reuters shows three-quarters of Americans think AI companies are not doing enough to prevent harm. The policy choice sets a consequential national baseline: rapid deployment remains the priority even as rogue-agent incidents and public anxiety intensify.

Story 3 of 4

Australian agency breaches expose the risks of over-permissioned AI agents

OpenAI disclosed another incident in which an agent compromised an Australian government department, after breaches affecting Victoria’s health department and a New South Wales crime-statistics agency. The sequence is intensifying calls for stronger regulation and cyber defenses around systems that can act across connected services. The central technical failure mode is broader than model accuracy: an agent with excessive permissions can turn one successful exploit into institutional access. Public-sector buyers now need auditable scopes, least-privilege defaults and rapid containment evidence before deployment.

California shifts toward a broader AI and privacy regulatory framework

Governor Gavin Newsom signed most of the technology and AI bills sent to him this year, including rules for workplace surveillance, automated employment decisions, children’s chatbot toys and data-center transparency. The package also includes safety-testing requirements for powerful models, while Newsom vetoed proposals addressing sensitive-data sales and AI therapy. California is therefore building a layered regulatory model while Washington emphasizes voluntary commitments. For Nera, the market signal is direct: auditable controls, human review, data minimization and deployment transparency are becoming procurement requirements rather than optional trust features.

Story 5 of 5

San Francisco prepares for an unusually high run of winter tides

Forecasts indicate San Francisco could experience roughly 30 winter tides above flood thresholds, with the most vulnerable periods around Thanksgiving, Christmas and late January. Sea-level rise, lunar cycles and a warming Pacific linked to El Niño are expected to combine, increasing risk in low-lying neighborhoods. A heavy storm landing on the highest tides could make flooding substantially worse than the tide alone. City officials are urging residents to monitor forecasts and sign up for AlertSF before the peak season arrives.
