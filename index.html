Iran says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until a seven-point deal is implemented

Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf says the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until seven conditions in a June interim agreement with the United States are fulfilled. Qatar is relaying messages between the sides, but Tehran says sequencing remains unresolved and insists the current talks concern the waterway rather than a broader nuclear bargain. The closure keeps a central energy artery inside an active geopolitical dispute and reinforces the risk premium already embedded in oil near $100 a barrel. Iran also warned that it prefers diplomacy but is prepared to answer further military action.

China accelerates construction of a runway on disputed Antelope Reef

Satellite imagery shows Chinese work advancing on a runway at Antelope Reef in the Paracel Islands, with analysts estimating completion by mid-2027. Vietnam and Taiwan also claim the area, so the project adds a durable strategic fact rather than a temporary patrol. The construction underscores how incremental infrastructure can change the regional balance without a single dramatic confrontation.

U.S. job growth slows sharply, sending stocks higher and the dollar lower

Employers added 29,000 jobs in September, far below the 90,000 gain economists surveyed by Reuters expected, while August was revised down to 133,000. By Friday afternoon the Nasdaq was up about 1.1%, the S&P 500 about 0.7% and the dollar weaker as traders reduced the chance of an October Federal Reserve increase to roughly one in five. Treasury yields reversed their initial decline, showing that the rate outlook remains unsettled even after the payroll miss. The market welcomed a possible pause, but the weak hiring number raises a less comfortable question about economic momentum entering the fourth quarter.

The White House is promoting a voluntary safety accord with Nvidia, SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and Alphabet rather than binding federal rules. The practical test is whether the group produces enforceable incident-response and accountability mechanisms or remains primarily a convening body.

OpenAI’s agent-hack review is costing $500,000 a day

OpenAI says its investigation into hacks involving its agents is costing roughly $500,000 per day and has required analysis of about 50 petabytes of data. More than 100 organizations have been notified, and a sixth Australian government site has been identified among the affected systems. The scale of the response shows how quickly an over-permissioned agent incident can become an expensive cross-institutional review. Buyers now need stronger evidence of least-privilege controls, auditability and containment before deploying autonomous systems across sensitive services.

San Francisco prepares for an unusually high run of winter tides

Forecasts indicate San Francisco could experience roughly 30 winter tides above flood thresholds, with the most vulnerable periods around Thanksgiving, Christmas and late January. Sea-level rise, lunar cycles and a warming Pacific linked to El Niño are expected to combine, increasing risk in low-lying neighborhoods. A heavy storm landing on the highest tides could make flooding substantially worse than the tide alone. City officials are urging residents to monitor forecasts and sign up for AlertSF before the peak season arrives.
