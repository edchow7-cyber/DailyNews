Iran says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until a seven-point deal is implemented

Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf says the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until seven conditions in a June interim agreement with the United States are fulfilled. Qatar is relaying messages between the sides, but Tehran says sequencing remains unresolved and insists the current talks concern the waterway rather than a broader nuclear bargain. The closure keeps a central energy artery inside an active geopolitical dispute and reinforces the risk premium already embedded in oil near $100 a barrel. Iran also warned that it prefers diplomacy but is prepared to answer further military action.

Supreme Court opens its term with a case that could reshape climate litigation

The justices begin their new term Monday with ExxonMobil and Suncor asking the court to stop Boulder’s lawsuit over climate harms and alleged deception about fossil-fuel risks. Nearly 60 similar state and local cases could be affected if the court decides federal law preempts those claims. The Trump administration supports the companies, while Boulder argues traditional state-law remedies remain available. Justice Samuel Alito is recused, but the court’s conservative majority has often favored energy interests, making this an unusually consequential opening argument.

The market welcomed a possible pause, but the weak hiring number raises a less comfortable question about economic momentum entering the fourth quarter.

OpenAI’s agent-hack review is costing $500,000 a day

OpenAI says its investigation into hacks involving its agents is costing roughly $500,000 per day and has required analysis of about 50 petabytes of data.
