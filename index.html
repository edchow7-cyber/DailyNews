Kyiv petrol-station strikes raise the toll in the expanding drone war

Russian jet-powered drones struck two petrol stations in Kyiv on Friday, killing two people and injuring 12, including a child, according to Ukrainian officials. A residential building and offices used by mobile operator Kyivstar were also hit. Ukraine’s own overnight strikes killed three people in Russia’s Tula and Kursk regions and again targeted oil and industrial infrastructure. The updated toll makes the morning exchange more consequential than the first overnight reports and shows both sides deepening attacks on economic as well as military targets.

Houthi forces seize a strategic island at the Bab el-Mandeb

Iran-aligned Houthi forces captured Perim Island and the Yemeni coastal town of Dhubab, strengthening their position around the Bab el-Mandeb shipping lane. The gain matters because the strait connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and has become even more important as Hormuz traffic remains constrained. Satellite imagery also showed smoke near Saudi Arabia’s East-West oil pipeline, though the cause was not confirmed. The advance gives Iran’s regional network another way to threaten energy and commercial shipping while Gulf governments search for a negotiated pause.

IEA warns the 2026 oil-supply gap will deepen

The International Energy Agency now expects global oil supply to fall by 5.7 million barrels per day this year as Gulf disruptions and attacks on tankers and facilities persist. Saudi production has dropped to about 6 million barrels per day, its lowest level in more than three decades, while refined-fuel inventories are being drawn down rapidly. The deterioration comes with Brent near $110 and bond markets already pricing a renewed inflation threat. Even with demand weakening, the agency’s revision points to sustained pressure on fuel prices, transport costs, and central-bank policy until normal Gulf flows return.

Tencent-backed Enflame jumps 179% in its Shanghai debut

Chinese AI-chip maker Enflame closed 179% above its offer price after raising $912 million on Shanghai’s STAR Market. The loss-making company is one of China’s “four little GPU dragons” and is positioned as a domestic alternative to Nvidia as Beijing pushes for greater technology independence. Tencent remains both its largest shareholder and dominant customer, accounting for most of Enflame’s 2025 revenue. The debut shows that capital is still willing to pay aggressively for AI infrastructure exposure even as broader Chinese equity indexes fell.

China’s Z.AI launches a $5 billion share-and-bond sale

Beijing-based model developer Z.AI launched a roughly $2 billion Hong Kong share placement alongside a $3 billion convertible-bond sale. The company, formerly known as Zhipu AI, says the proceeds will fund model research, computing capacity, infrastructure, acquisitions, and talent. It already raised about $4 billion in July, illustrating how quickly frontier-model economics consume capital. The transaction also sharpens the contrast between China’s public-market financing path and the giant private valuations being pursued by leading U.S. AI labs.

California enacts a sweeping child-tech package

Governor Gavin Newsom signed 13 bills aimed at reducing digital harms to minors. The package restricts addictive features such as infinite scroll and autoplay for users under 16, pauses sales of AI-chatbot toys for four years, and expands criminal penalties involving AI-generated child sexual-abuse material. It also adds parental-control and safety-assessment requirements for chatbot products. The laws are among the broadest state efforts yet to regulate both social platforms and consumer AI around children, though civil-liberties groups argue portions may be overly restrictive.

Model extraction turns confidential AI into a board-level control issue

Anthropic’s account of large-scale distillation attacks shows that proprietary AI can leak through legitimate-looking interfaces even when model weights and training data never leave the provider. Enterprises face the same pattern with internal copilots and specialized models: repeated queries can expose sensitive logic, derived information, and valuable behavior. Encryption at rest and in transit cannot address leakage that occurs while the system is actively computing. The control plane therefore needs identity, query policy, rate-pattern detection, and ways to minimize what any model or caller can see during execution.

Why it matters to Nera: This strengthens Nera’s case for protecting computation and derived results—not only raw inputs—and gives finance, healthcare, and IP-heavy buyers a concrete model-security story.

→ Reuters — Anthropic disrupts extraction campaigns

Story 2 of 2

Finance-specific AI raises the bar for enforceable data boundaries

OpenAI launched a financial-services version of ChatGPT built for investment banking and equity research, with integrations spanning market data, private-company information, financial models, and firm-specific templates. The product emphasizes role-based access, encryption, compliance logs, and connections to existing data subscriptions. Those controls are important, but financial workflows often combine material nonpublic information, licensed datasets, and internal analysis during the same computation. As specialized agents move from summarization into modeling and client deliverables, firms will need technical guarantees about which inputs can be combined, what each user may learn, and how derived outputs are exposed.

Why it matters to Nera: This is a direct market signal for confidential computation in FP&A, banking, and private equity—the same buyers Nera is targeting—where permissions and encryption must remain meaningful while sensitive data is actively processed.

→ Reuters — ChatGPT for financial services

Walker Kessler says a lost season renewed his appetite to win

Walker Kessler says missing nearly all of last season after shoulder surgery changed his perspective and made him fall in love with basketball again. If Kessler is physically right, his defense may be the single roster addition most capable of changing the Lakers’ ceiling.

BART blames heat and aging equipment for a third day of disruption

BART apologized after three days of major disruptions linked to extreme heat and aging infrastructure. A failed HVAC unit in a roughly 40-year-old Daly City traction-power substation contributed to a fire that damaged train-control equipment and a power cable. Most lines returned to normal schedules, but Green Line service remained suspended and Peninsula trains continued to run at reduced speeds while crews worked overnight. For riders heading to SFO or traveling along the Peninsula, the practical message is that service is operating but still fragile, with no firm timeline for full restoration.

The 49ers rout the Rams in the NFL’s first Australia game

San Francisco beat the favored Rams 27–7 before more than 100,000 fans at the Melbourne Cricket Ground. Brock Purdy threw three touchdown passes, the defense held Matthew Stafford to 155 passing yards, and a fourth-and-goal stop set up a decisive 99-yard scoring drive. The 49ers’ decision to arrive a week early appeared to pay off against a Rams team that landed only about 28 hours before kickoff. It is an unusually strong opening statement for a healthier San Francisco roster—and a striking example of preparation becoming a competitive advantage.
