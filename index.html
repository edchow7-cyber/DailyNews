Top Headlines

Democrats seize both chambers in a sweeping midterm repudiation

Democrats won control of the House and Senate in Tuesday's election, flipping seats even in long-standing Republican strongholds. Voters' frustration over the war with Iran, the cost of living, and President Trump's leadership produced a sharp national swing. Once the new Congress is seated in January, divided government will give Democrats subpoena power, control of the legislative agenda, and the ability to block nominees. The scale of the result also reduces the chance that control of Congress will remain tied up in prolonged recounts or litigation.

Source: Reuters, November 4

Russian strike on a Ukrainian aid centre leaves 37 dead and 59 missing

Rescuers continued searching a former school in Komyshuvakha after a Russian strike on the humanitarian-aid site killed at least 37 people. Ukrainian officials said 59 others were missing and that rescue workers and police officers were among the dead. Russia said it had targeted military units in the area, while Ukraine said civilians had gathered to collect aid; both countries deny deliberately targeting civilians. The attack came just after a limited evacuation ceasefire expired and before planned U.S.-backed talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11.

Source: Reuters, November 4

Business

Markets recover from an early election wobble as investors turn to AI oversight and rates

U.S. equity futures and global markets recovered early losses after the Democratic sweep, suggesting much of the outcome had already been priced in. The dollar remained weaker and Treasury yields slipped as investors weighed likely gridlock against stronger congressional oversight of AI, crypto, and data-centre development. September job openings rose sharply, supporting expectations for another Federal Reserve rate increase by year-end. AMD's earnings beat and strong AI-infrastructure demand helped lift technology shares despite the new regulatory uncertainty.

Source: Reuters, November 4

AMD's OpenAI and Meta chip agreements trigger a $1 billion quarterly charge

AMD recorded a roughly $1 billion accounting charge tied to warrants and other incentives in major AI-chip agreements with OpenAI and Meta. The cost underscores how aggressively chipmakers are competing to secure anchor customers in the data-centre market. Investors nevertheless pushed AMD shares higher after the company beat forecasts and affirmed strong AI demand. The tension is clear: strategic contracts can establish market share, but the economics must eventually justify large up-front concessions.

Source: Reuters Technology, November 4

Tech

Oracle cancels a $7 billion Tencent AI deal under U.S. pressure

Oracle abandoned a proposed multibillion-dollar AI-infrastructure agreement with Tencent after pressure from the Trump administration over national-security concerns. The decision illustrates how cloud capacity, advanced chips, and model training have become inseparable from export controls and geopolitical policy. It also removes a large potential customer while signaling that U.S. providers may face tighter scrutiny over Chinese AI relationships. Enterprise buyers should expect supplier geography and government policy to remain core architecture constraints.

Source: Reuters Technology, November 4

SK Hynix's New York chip plans run into new China restrictions

SK Hynix's planned U.S. expansion faces complications as Washington tightens restrictions affecting technology and operations linked to China. The company sits at the centre of the high-bandwidth-memory supply chain that supports modern AI accelerators. New controls can alter equipment access, production planning, and the economics of locating advanced manufacturing in the United States. The episode shows how quickly geopolitical rules can become a physical bottleneck for AI infrastructure.

Source: Reuters Technology, November 4

Nera Watch

Protegrity unifies policy enforcement across data, agents, and inference

Protegrity released a Cedar-based policy engine intended to apply centralized controls across enterprise data, AI agents, and model inference. The update also adds an expanded PII model, data and AI fast lanes, and an AI Gateway. By pushing the same policy definition into several layers, the company is trying to reduce gaps between data security and runtime AI governance. The launch makes context-aware enforcement a competitive product category rather than a collection of disconnected controls.

Why this is important to Nera: Protegrity is an adjacent enterprise competitor offering a unified control plane around confidential AI workflows. Nera should compare its own policy model, inference protection, PII coverage, and gateway integrations against this release, then sharpen differentiation around cryptographic guarantees and FHE rather than relying only on access policy.

Source: Protegrity, November 3

California privacy board prepares to consider opt-out signal rules

The California Privacy Protection Agency board meets November 5–6 with privacy rights, legislative updates, and regulatory proposals on the agenda. Meeting materials include work on opt-out preference signals, often called OOPS, and may determine whether draft changes advance into formal rulemaking and public comment. The work could strengthen how businesses recognize browser- or device-level opt-out choices and how they report compliance. Companies should treat the meeting as a decision point, not as a final rule already in force.

Why this is important to Nera: Stronger opt-out enforcement raises the value of systems that minimize raw-data exposure while preserving useful analytics. Nera can help customers separate consent state from sensitive records, enforce purpose limits inside encrypted computation, and produce auditable evidence that preference signals propagate through AI and data pipelines.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency, November 4

Belfort opens a GPU-accelerated encrypted-computing beta for PyTorch models

Belfort released EML, a platform designed to translate users' PyTorch models into encrypted computation and execute them with GPU acceleration. The company positions the beta as a practical route to running AI on sensitive data without decrypting it. Its compiler workflow and hardware focus aim to reduce the specialist effort and latency that have limited fully homomorphic encryption deployments. The release is an important sign that FHE vendors are moving from bespoke demonstrations toward developer-facing model workflows.

Why this is important to Nera: Belfort is a direct competitor in privacy-preserving AI and is setting buyer expectations around bring-your-own-model support, GPU latency, and developer usability. Nera should benchmark comparable models, document where its stack wins on accuracy, policy, deployment, and total cost, and accelerate customer proofs before encrypted-model execution becomes a crowded default feature.

Source: Belfort, October 20

Lakers / NBA

Dončić's 42-point night sends the Lakers north with a 6–2 record

Luka Dončić scored 42 points and added 13 assists as Los Angeles beat Miami 138–117 on Monday. Austin Reaves scored 27, and Walker Kessler added a 15-point, 15-rebound double-double. The Lakers shot 57.3% and broke the game open with an 11–0 run to start the third quarter. The win gave Los Angeles a 6–2 record and one of the league's most productive early-season offenses.

Source: Reuters, November 3

Lakers visit Sacramento tonight as the Kings search for answers

The Lakers face the Kings at Golden 1 Center tonight at 7 p.m. Pacific. Los Angeles enters 6–2, while Sacramento is 1–6 after a difficult opening stretch. The matchup will test whether the Lakers' shot-making and improved rebounding travel, and whether they can reduce the turnovers that have survived during recent wins. It also starts a road-and-cup sequence that continues Friday, giving the game more value than a routine early-November stop.

Source: Los Angeles Lakers, November 4

Bay Area

Bay Area voters approve regional and San Francisco transit lifelines

Voters approved the Regional Transit Measure and San Francisco's Proposition H, delivering new revenue to public transportation systems facing severe operating gaps. RTM is expected to raise about $1 billion annually across five counties for agencies including BART, Muni, Caltrain, and AC Transit. Proposition H adds a separate San Francisco parcel tax focused on Muni. The wins avert an immediate service-collapse scenario, but agencies still face difficult decisions about spending, accountability, and long-term ridership.

Source: KQED, November 4

Valkyries championship parade takes over San Francisco's waterfront today

San Francisco will celebrate the WNBA champion Golden State Valkyries with a parade from noon to 2 p.m. along the Embarcadero. SFMTA has announced transit changes, street closures, and restrictions around the route, and regional agencies are urging riders to allow extra time. The event is both a civic celebration and a statement about how quickly the expansion franchise has built a Bay Area following. Fans are encouraged to use transit and check live service updates rather than drive into the affected area.

Source: SFMTA, November 4
