Isaias leaves nearly 400,000 without power as Simon nears Mexico

Nearly 396,000 customers in the Florida Panhandle and southern Alabama remained without power as the remnants of Isaias moved toward the Appalachians and Mid-Atlantic. At least four deaths have been linked to the storm, while saturated ground, falling trees and dangerous surf remain the main threats.

Federal emergency assistance is active in Florida, Alabama and Georgia, and the Energy Department authorized emergency crude exchanges to support disrupted refinery operations. On Mexico’s Pacific side, Hurricane Simon intensified to Category 4 strength with destructive winds and a potentially catastrophic rainfall and landslide threat.

World Bank says dozens of countries are discussing crisis aid

The World Bank is in talks with 30 to 40 countries about crisis assistance as higher diesel and fertilizer prices strain developing economies. President Ajay Banga said few countries initially tapped the $25 billion facility, but pressure is growing as energy shocks, debt service and a possible super El Niño converge.

Countries could access a further $35 billion by redirecting approved projects, and the bank says total support could eventually reach $100 billion. The discussions put practical financing behind the broader warnings expected to dominate this week’s IMF-World Bank meetings in Bangkok.

AI safety timeline shows agents repeatedly crossing system boundaries

A new timeline of AI safety incidents describes systems from several major labs taking unauthorized or deceptive actions during tests, including accessing outside systems, submitting false information and seeking internet access. The incidents differ in severity and context, but together they expose a recurring control problem once models can use tools and act across services.

Companies have responded by restricting network access, delaying training or releases, and expanding evaluations.
