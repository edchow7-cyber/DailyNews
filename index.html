Top Headlines

Ukraine says Starlink restrictions have ended

Ukraine says Starlink and the military Starshield service are again operating normally after more than a week of throttling. The restrictions disrupted military operations and intensified a broader deterioration in relations between Kyiv and Washington. Restoring service removes an immediate operational constraint, but it also demonstrates how dependent battlefield communications remain on a privately controlled network and shifting U.S. policy.

Jordan church attack toll rises to eight

Eight people were killed when gunmen attacked a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, Jordanian authorities said. More than two dozen people were treated in hospitals, and security was increased after officials described the assault as extremist terrorism. The rare attack on Jordan's Christian minority raises immediate questions about the perpetrators, their network, and whether further communal targets face heightened risk.

Russia and Ukraine agree to a local humanitarian ceasefire

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a ceasefire around occupied Oleshky beginning October 27 to allow aid deliveries and civilian evacuations. The arrangement is geographically narrow, while both sides continue long-range attacks elsewhere and prepare for another round of talks. Its real significance will depend on implementation: a functioning corridor could establish a small template for practical agreements even when a broader settlement remains distant.

The split matters because robust headline growth will not automatically translate into household income or broad employment gains.

Tech

Moonshot limits Kimi's offensive cyber capability

Chinese startup Moonshot removed cyber-offensive training data and reinforcement environments from the open-weight version of its new Kimi model. The company says the change reduced controlled vulnerability-exploitation performance while preserving broad coding capability. The decision highlights a growing tension for open models: safety restrictions can reduce misuse, but weights distributed outside a hosted service remain difficult to monitor or retract.

AI-discovered vulnerabilities strain disclosure systems

Security researchers expect AI systems to find software flaws at a volume traditional disclosure and patching processes were not designed to handle. Defensive discovery can improve security, but it also lowers the cost for attackers searching for exploitable weaknesses. The critical advantage will belong to organizations that can validate, prioritize, and remediate findings faster than adversaries can operationalize them.

Meta's Muse exposed thousands of shared conversations

Researchers found more than 6,800 publicly accessible links created by Muse's conversation-sharing feature, including some with personal records and internal business material. Meta paused the feature after the issue drew outside scrutiny, but the incident shows how convenience controls can create unexpected discovery paths even without search-engine indexing. AI assistants magnify this risk because users often provide the very context—documents, plans, and personal details—that makes an assistant useful.

Fhenix brings FHE confidentiality to Arbitrum mainnet

Fhenix has launched its CoFHE encrypted-computation coprocessor on Arbitrum with two design partners. The system supports private balances and application logic without moving assets into a separate privacy network, and its first uses include compliance infrastructure and confidential trading strategies. Moving from testnet to production is an important category milestone, even though deployments initially remain permissioned and partner-reviewed.

Why this is important to Nera: Fhenix is a direct competitive benchmark and strong validation that buyers want usable FHE rather than cryptographic primitives alone. Nera Systems should compare developer experience, performance, governance, model support, and enterprise deployment pathways—and use the launch to sharpen its story for confidential AI workflows beyond blockchain.

The SEC puts AI on its 2027 examination agenda

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission says AI, private assets, and tokenized securities will be priorities in examinations of regulated financial firms. Examiners will assess how technology affects operations, compliance, fraud prevention, and investor protection. The announcement shifts AI governance from an abstract future concern toward a concrete supervisory question for investment advisers, broker-dealers, exchanges, and private funds.

Why this is important to Nera: Financial institutions will need evidence that sensitive prompts, portfolios, customer data, and model outputs are protected throughout processing. Nera can translate confidential AI and FHE into examination-ready controls—minimal plaintext exposure, separable key authority, auditable policy, and safer analytics across firms—while targeting compliance-driven pilots.

NIST's privacy-cryptography workshop opens with FHE

NIST's three-day Workshop on Privacy-Enhancing Cryptography begins Monday, October 26, with its entire first day devoted to fully homomorphic encryption. Sessions cover benchmarking, encrypted neural networks, differential privacy under FHE, and private search. The agenda shows that the standards and research community is moving beyond whether FHE works toward how systems should be measured, composed, and deployed.

Why this is important to Nera: Benchmark definitions and credible evaluation methods directly affect enterprise trust and procurement. Nera Systems should track the workshop's metrics and implementation lessons, align product evidence with emerging public standards, and identify speakers or institutions that could strengthen validation, partnerships, and customer education.

A 39-point third quarter broke open a tied game, while 26 forced turnovers produced 33 Lakers points. Bay Area

San Francisco declares an El Niño emergency

Mayor Daniel Lurie declared a local emergency ahead of a historically strong El Niño expected to bring heavy rain, wind, high tides, and coastal flooding. The declaration speeds purchases of pumps, barriers, and generators, while the city prepares waterfront defenses, clears drains, and expands sandbag distribution. The first major storm window may arrive around Halloween, giving residents and agencies a short period to prepare before repeated winter systems test low-lying infrastructure.

Bird flu is detected at an East Bay beach

Canada geese collected at Crown Memorial State Beach in Alameda tested positive for highly pathogenic avian influenza. Officials say the general-public risk remains low, but visitors should avoid sick or dead birds and keep children and dogs away from birds, carcasses, and droppings. Fall migration can increase spread among waterfowl, so reports and continued monitoring matter even without evidence of person-to-person transmission.

Northern California's first storm brings beneficial rain and lightning

A rotating low-pressure system produced rain and more than 24,000 lightning detections across Northern California, with most strikes remaining in the atmosphere. Wetting rain reduced immediate ignition risk, though forecasters cautioned that dry fuels mean fire season is not finished. Drier and gradually warmer weekend conditions offer a pause before the stronger El Niño pattern expected later in the month.
