Nera Watch

FTC subpoenas OpenAI and Anthropic over consumer harms

The FTC has issued broad civil subpoenas seeking information on safety incidents, security controls, privacy policies, and marketing claims, Reuters reports. The inquiry appears focused on whether AI companies have adequately guarded against consumer harm, including damage caused by agents during security testing. The investigation could turn voluntary safety promises into evidence tested under consumer-protection law.

Why this is important to Nera: This creates a direct market opening for auditable privacy and safety controls. Nera can frame confidential AI and privacy-preserving computation not as optional infrastructure, but as evidence that customers can show regulators when explaining how sensitive data and agent behavior are controlled.

Cohere's AISLE move brings cyber defense into the enterprise-AI stack

Cohere's planned purchase of AISLE would combine an enterprise model vendor with an AI-focused cybersecurity team. The strategic logic is clear: regulated customers increasingly want one accountable architecture for models, data, identity, and defense. Consolidation may speed adoption, but it also raises the bar for independent privacy vendors that need crisp integration stories.

Why this is important to Nera: Cohere is both a potential partner ecosystem and a stronger adjacent competitor for enterprise trust budgets. Nera should emphasize the part AISLE does not automatically solve: keeping inputs, model interactions, and collaborative analytics confidential through PETs and FHE rather than relying only on perimeter security.

Higher capital costs force AI infrastructure buyers to prove value

The selloff in AI equities and credit is pressuring data-center projects whose economics depend on sustained demand and inexpensive financing. Buyers will ask harder questions about utilization, vendor lock-in, and the cost of moving sensitive data into centralized environments. That scrutiny can slow broad platform commitments while favoring targeted deployments with measurable business value.

Why this is important to Nera: Confidential AI can reduce the need to duplicate or centralize sensitive data, potentially lowering both risk and integration cost. Nera's near-term opportunity is to quantify that economic benefit for customers, not only the cryptographic benefit, as CFOs gain influence over AI architecture.

International AI oversight proposal points toward common evaluation rules

Scientists proposing a global oversight body want shared monitoring of advanced systems whose risks do not stop at borders. Any workable regime will need repeatable evaluations and ways to inspect systems without exposing proprietary or personal data. That tension—more oversight with less unnecessary disclosure—is exactly where privacy-enhancing technologies can become enabling infrastructure.

Why this is important to Nera: FHE and related PETs could let companies participate in cross-border audits, benchmarks, and incident analysis while protecting model IP and regulated datasets. Nera should track emerging evaluation standards early so its product can map to the evidence regulators and enterprise buyers will actually request.
