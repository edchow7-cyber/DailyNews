Story 4 of 5

A judge ends 23 years of Oakland police oversight

U.S. District Judge William Orrick ended federal monitoring of the Oakland Police Department after 23 years, closing the longest-running oversight of a U.S. police force. The process began with a 2003 civil-rights settlement tied to the Riders misconduct scandal and ultimately required compliance with 51 reforms. City leaders called the decision a milestone, while civil-rights advocates emphasized that accountability must continue through local institutions. The next test is whether the department can preserve investigative discipline and public trust without the court’s external pressure.

Story 5 of 5

Appeals court protects Bay Area journalist’s reporting

A California appeals court rejected a lawsuit against Bay Area technology journalist Jack Poulson over his reporting on a tech executive’s sealed arrest record. The judges found Poulson did not know the record had been sealed when an anonymous source provided it and concluded that the report addressed a matter of public interest because the executive led a government contractor. The ruling reinforces constitutional protection for publishing truthful information lawfully obtained by a journalist. A further appeal remains possible, but the decision is an important local press-freedom precedent for reporting on surveillance and government-adjacent technology firms.
