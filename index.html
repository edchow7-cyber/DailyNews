Saudi pipeline shutdown deepens the two-chokepoint oil crisis

Saudi Arabia shut its East–West oil pipeline after a drone attack, interrupting a route capable of moving roughly 4–5 million barrels a day around the Strait of Hormuz. At the same time, Houthi forces consolidated control of Perim Island at the entrance to the Bab el-Mandeb, tightening pressure on the Red Sea route. Riyadh has asked Washington for additional help while investigating an Iraqi launch point, and Iraq has reinforced its border. The combined disruption threatens two routes that together underpin a large share of global energy trade, with oil already above $100 a barrel.

House prepares a sweeping Russia-sanctions vote

The U.S. House plans to take up a Russia sanctions bill next week after the Senate passed it 86–11. The measure would tighten pressure on Moscow and authorize steep tariffs against countries that continue buying Russian energy. Its bipartisan momentum makes passage more plausible than earlier sanctions pushes. If enacted, the secondary-tariff provisions could reorder energy trade and compound price pressure while Gulf and Red Sea supplies are already strained.

Investors pull $15.5 billion from global equity funds

Global equity funds recorded $15.52 billion of net outflows in the week through September 9, their largest weekly redemption since June. U.S. funds alone lost $32.27 billion as the oil shock, inflation worries, and higher bond yields pushed investors away from risk. Bond and money-market products still attracted cash, showing that the move is defensive rather than a broad liquidity flight. The scale of the U.S. selling is an early signal that the energy shock is beginning to reshape portfolio positioning.

Central banks confront a fresh oil-and-inflation test

The coming week puts monetary policy back under pressure as oil above $100 feeds inflation expectations and bond yields. Markets are weighing whether the Federal Reserve under Kevin Warsh may need to tighten, while the Bank of Japan is expected to consider raising its policy rate to 1.25%. Investors will also watch a proposed BRICS central-bank-digital-currency settlement link and Sweden’s election. The common thread is that energy disruption is narrowing policymakers’ room to support growth without reigniting inflation.

A lawyer is fined after ChatGPT invents witnesses

New Mexico’s Supreme Court fined defense lawyer Stephen Aarons $5,000 and held him in contempt after an appellate brief included fabricated police testimony and witnesses. Aarons said he used ChatGPT to summarize trial proceedings and failed to verify the result. The appeal concerns a client serving a life sentence, making the lapse more than a routine citation error. The case is a sharp reminder that professional AI workflows need source-grounded review and accountability.

Senate negotiators weigh an AI duty of care

U.S. senators are negotiating legislation that would require frontier-model developers to mitigate known catastrophic risks before release. The proposal could let the federal government block an unsafe model, subject to court challenge, and may preempt some state rules. National-laboratory testing is also under discussion for risks involving nuclear, biological, cyber, and autonomous capabilities. The bill is unsettled, but it shifts the policy debate from voluntary promises toward auditable obligations tied to how powerful models behave.

Why it matters to Nera: A duty-of-care regime will create demand for enforceable evidence about data access, model behavior, policy compliance, and what derived information can leave a computation.

Story 2 of 2

Claude abuse maps the widening AI threat surface

Anthropic says actors attempted to use Claude for biological-weapons research, vulnerability discovery, surveillance, cyber operations, and industrialized romance fraud. One campaign targeted 42 European entities; another deployed thousands of AI personas across more than 20 dating apps. Many operators bypassed geographic restrictions, exposing the limits of account-level controls once sensitive models are broadly reachable. The incidents show that identity, intent, data access, and model behavior have to be governed together.

Why it matters to Nera: Buyers need enforcement around the computation itself—who can combine which data with which model and what derived information can leave—not just perimeter access and encrypted storage.

BART’s Fremont corridor closes for weekend track work

BART will run no trains between Union City and Warm Springs on September 12–13 while crews replace interlocking equipment near Fremont Station. The Green Line is suspended, Fremont Station has no train service, and free buses bridge the closed segment. Riders should allow 30–40 extra minutes; Orange Line trains operate as a southern shuttle while the Blue, Red, and Yellow lines keep their normal intervals. The planned shutdown replaces Friday’s all-clear as the most actionable transit update for the weekend.
