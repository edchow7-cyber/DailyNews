Top Headlines

Wars in Ukraine and the Middle East fracture the G20 agenda

Foreign ministers gathering in Atlanta face deep disagreements over Ukraine, the Middle East, sanctions, and possible U.S. strikes on Iran. The U.S. wants trade imbalances, energy, supply chains, and critical minerals on the agenda, but a unified communique may be out of reach. Russia’s participation and South Africa’s exclusion add another layer of friction. Smaller coalitions and issue-specific statements may be the meeting’s most realistic output.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Mine incidents in the Strait of Hormuz raise shipping and oil risks

Iran says multiple oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz, while another tanker caught fire after explosives were attached to its hull. The reports pushed oil higher and intensified concern about a route central to global energy supply. Regional governments are also preparing for the possibility of renewed U.S.–Iran military action after the American midterms. Even a short disruption could compound inflation and shipping-insurance costs.

Source: Associated Press, October 30

Oleshky ceasefire creates a narrow opening for humanitarian relief

A localized ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky has held while crews clear mines from access roads. The mission aims to deliver food, medicine, and water to roughly 2,000 residents and evacuate civilians under Ukrainian, Russian, and U.S. observation. Fighting continues elsewhere, so the operation is not a wider truce. Still, sustained access would be a meaningful humanitarian result and a small test of future technical cooperation.

Source: Associated Press, October 28

Business

Tech outlooks pull futures lower as investors question AI spending

U.S. stock futures fell after cautious outlooks from Amazon and Intel joined the market’s negative response to Alphabet’s results. Nasdaq futures dropped more than 2%, while Intel was down sharply on a weak PC-market outlook. Investors are distinguishing companies that can finance AI infrastructure from operating cash flow from those producing prolonged cash burn. Oil’s rise above $100 adds a separate inflation concern.

Source: Associated Press, October 30

DoorDash agrees to acquire Instacart for about $17 billion

DoorDash will pay $65 per Instacart share, a 26% premium to the pre-rumor closing price. At least 75% of the consideration will be cash, and Instacart’s brand, app, and enterprise products are expected to remain. The companies argue that combining restaurant, grocery, retail, and merchant technology will help local stores compete with larger online platforms. The deal faces shareholder and regulatory review and is not expected to close until late 2027 or the first half of 2028.

Source: Instacart and DoorDash, October 29

Earnings, jobs data, and the midterms will test the AI trade again

Next week brings major earnings from Apple, AMD, and Palantir alongside the monthly jobs report and U.S. midterm elections. Alphabet’s cash burn and weaker growth have already shaken the hyperscaler-led rally. The election could also shift the outlook for AI regulation and federal spending. Markets now need both strong demand and credible financing discipline to keep the AI investment thesis intact.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Tech

AI is becoming central to U.S. warfare, but the failure modes are consequential

The Pentagon has expanded AI systems that combine intelligence feeds and commercial models to accelerate analysis and targeting. Officials say the tools help analysts synthesize more information, yet reported errors have nearly prompted wrongful interdiction and contributed to flawed assessments. Faster decision cycles do not guarantee strategic success or eliminate civilian harm. The deployment race is outpacing agreement on human oversight, auditability, and accountability.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Foxconn says another OpenAI collaboration is in development

Foxconn Chairman Young Liu says the company is preparing a new partnership with OpenAI after meeting Sam Altman. The relationship would build on earlier work designing data-center racks and components. AI servers now account for more than half of Foxconn’s cloud and networking revenue, and the company expects double-digit growth next year. Foxconn is also expanding its own use of humanoid robots in Texas manufacturing facilities.

Source: Reuters, October 30

Some companies are rehiring people after AI-driven cuts

Employers that cut staff to fund or deploy AI are discovering that chatbots and automation still need human judgment and institutional knowledge. Klarna resumed customer-service hiring, while Ford brought experienced engineers back into quality-control work. The reversals do not mean AI is disappearing; they point toward hybrid roles that combine domain expertise with automation. Workers asked to return have leverage to seek clearer responsibilities, training, and employment protections.

Source: Associated Press, October 30

Nera Watch

EU consultation on generative-AI web scraping closes today

The European Data Protection Board’s draft guidelines address legal bases for large-scale scraping, special-category data, transparency, and whether downstream data can truly be anonymous. The feedback window closes October 30. The Board’s related anonymization framework focuses on whether records can be isolated, linked, or used for inference. The final guidance could materially affect how model developers assemble and reuse training data in Europe.

Why this is important to Nera: Stricter scraping and anonymization tests increase demand for architectures that minimize plaintext exposure and provably constrain data use. Nera can position confidential AI and FHE as technical controls that support privacy-by-design, while being careful not to imply encryption alone resolves legal-basis or transparency obligations.

Source: European Data Protection Board

NIST’s privacy-enhancing cryptography workshop points toward combined systems

WPEC 2026 covered fully homomorphic encryption, private set intersection, zero-knowledge proofs, and privacy-preserving credentials across three days. Presentations emphasized benchmarking, encrypted neural networks, and deployment challenges rather than treating each primitive as an isolated research topic. The agenda shows the market moving toward systems that combine PETs around specific workflows. Operational evidence and interoperability will increasingly matter as much as cryptographic novelty.

Why this is important to Nera: Nera should translate FHE performance into workload-level benchmarks and show how it composes with identity, access, and audit controls. The workshop also provides a standards map and partner landscape for enterprise pilots, procurement evidence, and future product positioning.

Source: NIST WPEC 2026

Locktera uses BCaster to demonstrate an enterprise confidential-AI deployment

Locktera says BCaster is using its platform to protect sensitive AI image and video data with encryption, policy controls, and auditing. The case moves Locktera’s message beyond technology claims into a named customer workflow. It also broadens the competitive frame from encrypted retrieval to persistent governance around AI data files. Enterprise buyers may increasingly expect vendors to show both cryptographic protection and integration evidence.

Why this is important to Nera: Locktera is a direct confidential-AI competitor building customer proof and ecosystem credibility. Nera should compare trust models, FHE depth, performance, auditability, and ease of Excel/enterprise integration, then accelerate a reference deployment that demonstrates why “Nera never sees secrets” is operationally distinct.

Source: Locktera, October 21

Bay Area

Record-high water extends Bay Area flood advisories through Sunday

Coastal flood advisories remain in effect after water levels reached 2.2 feet above normal and receded more slowly than expected. The National Weather Service attributes the event to low air pressure and exceptionally warm Pacific waters associated with El Niño. Shoreline roads around San Pablo, San Francisco, Monterey, Sonoma, and Marin may close intermittently. Drivers should expect the highest risk around morning tide cycles and avoid water of unknown depth.

Source: San Francisco Chronicle, October 30

Valkyries lose Game 6 and return home for a winner-take-all finale

Atlanta beat Golden State 80–69 behind 23 points from Allisha Gray and 21 from Rhyne Howard. The Valkyries trailed by 25 in the first quarter before Gabby Williams led a second-half rally, but they never fully closed the gap. The WNBA Finals are now tied 3–3. Game 7 is Saturday at 5 p.m. Pacific at Chase Center.

Source: Golden State Valkyries, October 29

Justice Department subpoenas California election officials

Federal subpoenas order Los Angeles County election employees to appear November 4 and produce records related to alleged noncitizen voting. Santa Clara and other counties have also received demands as the administration expands its election investigations. A judge previously rejected a request to segregate roughly 20,000 ballots and warned against threatening election workers. Available evidence indicates noncitizen voting is rare, but the legal and operational dispute is escalating days before the midterms.

Source: Associated Press, October 30
